Does yoga count as strength training? What guidelines and trials show

How official activity guidance classes yoga, what controlled trials measured for strength, and how those results sit next to the research on progressive resistance training.

By Susmit Jalan, strength and conditioning coach at Online Fitness Worldwide. Updated 21 November 2026

The NHS lists yoga among its examples of muscle-strengthening activities for adults, alongside pilates, lifting weights and bodyweight exercises.[1] WHO's 2020 guidelines recommend muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days a week.[2]

In trials, yoga improved measured strength against controls. A 2019 meta-analysis of randomised trials in adults aged 60 and over found effects favouring yoga for lower-limb strength compared with inactive controls (effect size 0.45) and active controls such as walking (0.49).[3] A 12-week hatha yoga trial in 173 adults reported significant gains in push-up and curl-up tests against a waitlist group.[4]

The research on progressive resistance training reports separate findings: a 2026 ACSM position stand reports that strength gains were enhanced by heavier loads (80% of one-repetition maximum or more). None of the cited yoga trials compared yoga with progressive weight training directly.[5]

Doses, comparisons and limits

In the hatha yoga trial, 87 adults were assigned to yoga and 86 to a waitlist; 19 dropped out. The yoga group also improved maximal oxygen uptake and sit-and-reach flexibility; the older subgroup did not improve the curl-up test compared with controls.[4]

The balance meta-analysis pooled 6 trials with 307 participants and states that further research is required to determine whether the improvements translate to fall prevention.[6] The ACSM position stand pooled 137 systematic reviews of resistance training in healthy adults; it reports strength enhanced by loads of 80% of one-repetition maximum or more, a full range of motion, 2 to 3 sets and 2 or more sessions a week.[5]

What the guidelines and sources state on safety

ACSM's 2015 screening model bases pre-exercise medical clearance on current activity, signs or symptoms and/or known cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease, and desired intensity.[7]

Frequently asked questions

Is yoga listed as a muscle-strengthening activity?

Yes. The NHS lists yoga among examples of muscle-strengthening activities for adults, with pilates, tai chi, lifting weights and bodyweight exercises.[1]

Has yoga improved strength in trials?

A meta-analysis in adults aged 60 and over found effects favouring yoga for lower-limb strength against inactive and active controls.[3] A 12-week hatha yoga trial reported significant gains in push-up and curl-up tests against a waitlist group.[4]

Has yoga been compared with weight training?

None of the trials cited here compared yoga with progressive weight training. A 2026 ACSM position stand reports that strength gains in resistance training were enhanced by loads of 80% of one-repetition maximum or more.[5]

What did studies find for balance?

A meta-analysis of 6 trials in people aged 60 and over found a small effect of yoga on balance and a medium effect on physical mobility.[6]
