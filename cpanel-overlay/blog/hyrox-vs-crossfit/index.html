HYROX vs CrossFit: 7 differences that matter before you choose

HYROX is a fixed fitness race against the clock. CrossFit is a training method, and its Open is a three-week online competition. Here is how they differ, and how to train for both.

By Susmit Jalan, coach at Online Fitness Worldwide. Updated 2 November 2026

You want to know which one to try. Or whether you can do both.

They are not the same kind of thing. HYROX is a race: the same eight stations and 8 km of running, everywhere.[1] CrossFit is a training method, and its yearly competition is the Open.[3][4]

So I compare the HYROX race with the CrossFit Open. Facts come from each organiser's own pages, read on 2 November 2026.

The 2027 Open workouts are not published yet. I won't guess them.[4]

Independent comparison. Not an official post from either brand.

  1. 1A fixed course, or workouts you don't know yet

    The format
    HYROX: 1 km run, then one station, eight times. Same stations, same order, in every city.[1]
    What is different
    CrossFit Open: three workouts over three weeks. They are released on the CrossFit Games website on Wednesdays.[4] Until then you don't know what they are.
    A good fit if
    You like to know the test and train for it: HYROX. You like a surprise: the Open.

  2. 2How you are scored

    The format
    HYROX: your finish time. Two-minute penalties apply, for example for the wrong station.[1][2]
    What is different
    Open: you do the workout, then submit your score online by Monday 5 p.m. PT. Leaderboards show you by gym, age group or worldwide.[4]
    A good fit if
    CrossFit's pages don't say yet how each 2027 workout is scored. I won't guess.[4]

  3. 3The movements you need

    The format
    HYROX: SkiErg, sled push, sled pull, burpee broad jumps, row, farmers carry, sandbag lunges, wall balls, plus 8 km of running.[1]
    What is different
    CrossFit's Open page lists snatches, thrusters, muscle-ups, toes-to-bar, handstand push-ups and double-unders as examples of firsts people hit in the Open.[4] These are examples. They are not the 2027 workouts. New to Olympic lifts like the snatch or clean? Don't teach yourself at home from videos. Learn them with a coach watching, starting with a PVC pipe or an empty bar. At home, train the pattern with dumbbell or kettlebell hinges and push presses instead.
    A good fit if
    Skills like muscle-ups need planned practice over time. HYROX has none of them.

  4. 4Where you do it

    The format
    HYROX: at an event venue on race day. On 2 November 2026 the finder listed 66 events, with 36 country options.[9]
    What is different
    Open: at your CrossFit gym, or wherever you can do the workout and log the score. CrossFit says it has more than 9,000 affiliate gyms worldwide.[3]
    A good fit if
    Registration for the 2027 Open opens on 7 January 2027.[4] HYROX entry is per event. Check the event page.

  5. 5The path after the first step

    The format
    HYROX: age groups from 16. The rulebook gives each race class at each event one qualification spot for the World Championships, in Hong Kong, 10 to 13 June 2027.[2][9]
    What is different
    CrossFit: Open, then Quarterfinals (the top 25% worldwide), then Semifinals (the top 2,000 men and 2,000 women), then the Games (the top 40 men and 40 women).[4]
    A good fit if
    Both say they welcome everyone. HYROX says it is for everybody.[1] CrossFit says anyone can join the Open, whatever their fitness level.[4]

  6. 6What training they share

    The format
    Both reward strength and an engine. The WHO says adults should do 150 to 300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week, plus muscle-strengthening on 2 or more days.[7]
    What is different
    A meta-analysis of 43 studies found adding aerobic work made no clear difference to maximal strength or muscle size, but explosive strength was lower, more so when both were in the same session.[6]
    A good fit if
    So I spread the hard days. Lift and run on different days when you can.

  7. 7Doing both in one year

    The format
    The 2027 Open runs 17 February to 8 March.[4][5] HYROX's finder lists events up to 10 June 2027.[9]
    What is different
    A simple plan: use the three Open weeks as a strength and skill test. Then switch to a running base and the stations for a spring HYROX.
    A good fit if
    I can't promise a result from any plan. No client results here, and none implied.

A sample week with the Open in it

Sample only. Not a prescription. Adjust it to your body, your health and your history.

  • Mon: lift, squat and press, 3 sets of 5 to 8.
  • Tue: easy run, 30 minutes.
  • Wed: Open workout day. Warm up for 15 minutes first.
  • Thu: rest or a walk.
  • Fri: lift, hinge and pull, 3 sets of 8.
  • Sat: 5 km easy run, then 4 rounds of wall balls and a carry.
  • Sun: rest. Adjust it to your body and your history.

Before you start

General information, not medical advice. ACSM's screening model says: if you are inactive, have no symptoms and no known heart, metabolic or kidney disease, you can start light to moderate exercise without medical clearance and build up gradually. If you have one of those conditions, or symptoms such as chest pain, unusual breathlessness or dizziness, get medical clearance first.[8] Race-style work is hard: heavy lifts, jumps, burpees, sprints and carries. Build up to it over weeks. Don't test a maximum lift alone or without a spotter or a coach watching. Heavy lifts, jumps, burpees and fast running are hard on knees, back and shoulders. Build up over weeks. Ask a doctor first if you have a heart, blood-pressure or joint condition.
