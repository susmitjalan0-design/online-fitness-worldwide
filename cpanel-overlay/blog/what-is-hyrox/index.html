What is HYROX? 7 things to know before your first race

HYROX is a fitness race. Eight runs of 1 km, each followed by a station. Same format in every city. Here is what the organiser publishes, and what third parties add.

By Susmit Jalan, coach at Online Fitness Worldwide. Updated 2 November 2026

HYROX is a fitness race. You run 1 km, then do one station. You do that 8 times.[1]

The format is the same everywhere. So a time from one city can be compared with a time from another.[1]

The organiser's India site says that over 80 global races in 2025 saw over 550,000 athletes and 350,000 spectators.[3] That is HYROX's own figure. I can't check it.

Weights are the 2026/27 season's. Kilograms first, pounds in brackets (rounded). Rules change. Read the current rulebook before you race.[2]

This is an independent explainer, not an official HYROX post.

  1. 1The format: 8 rounds, 8 km of running

    What it is
    Run about 1,000 m. Then one workout station. Eight times. The running totals 8,000 m.[1][2]
    What you do
    The area between the run and the stations is the Roxzone, a transition zone. The run course, the Roxzone and the stations together make the race.[2]
    Good to know
    Venues differ, so a 1 km run can be several laps. The rulebook says the full 8,000 m is still run.[2]

  2. 2The 8 stations, in order

    What it is
    SkiErg 1,000 m. Sled push 50 m. Sled pull 50 m. Burpee broad jumps 80 m. Row 1,000 m. Farmers carry 200 m. Sandbag lunges 100 m. Wall balls, 100 reps.[1][2]
    What you do
    HYROX says the SkiErg works arms, shoulders and core, the sled push works the lower body and the posterior chain, the sled pull works glutes, back, biceps and trunk, the farmers carry works upper back, core and grip, and the lunges work thighs and glutes.[1]
    Good to know
    Weights, Open women / Open men (also Pro women): sled push 102 / 152 kg (225 / 335 lb) including the sled; sled pull 78 / 103 kg (172 / 227 lb); carry 2 × 16 / 2 × 24 kg (35 / 53 lb); lunge bag 10 / 20 kg (22 / 44 lb); wall ball 4 / 6 kg (9 / 13 lb). Pro men: push 202 kg (445 lb), pull 153 kg (337 lb), carry 2 × 32 kg (71 lb), bag 30 kg (66 lb), ball 9 kg (20 lb).[2]

  3. 3Divisions: Open, Pro, Doubles, Relay

    What it is
    Open is the standard race. Pro uses heavier weights. In Doubles, two people run together and split each station as they choose. In Relay, a team of four each runs 2 × 1 km and does 1 station.[1]
    What you do
    Age groups: under 24 (16 to 24), then 5-year groups from 25-29 up to 85-89, by age on race day.[2] You must be at least 16 on race day.[2]
    Good to know
    HYROX says it is for everybody, from professional athletes to everyday gym-goers.[1]

  4. 4The rules that cost people time

    What it is
    Two-minute penalties apply, for example for using the wrong IN or OUT arch, or the wrong workout station.[2]
    What you do
    Wall balls: a full squat with hips below the knees, and the ball must hit the correct target. 2.70 m for women, 3.00 m for men. Anything else is a no-rep.[2]
    Good to know
    The rulebook is the only source that counts on the day. Read the current one at hyrox.com/rulebook.[2]

  5. 5What a typical finish looks like

    What it is
    A third-party analysis of 1,274,586 official finishes (RoxTimes): typical Open men 1:27:59, Open women 1:36:00. The fastest 10% finish under 1:10:40 (men) and 1:18:24 (women).[6]
    What you do
    In HYROX's own sports-science report, 11 recreational athletes simulated an Open race. The median was 86.2 minutes: 51.2 running, 32.8 on the stations. A tiny sample.[5]
    Good to know
    These are not targets. They do show one thing: the running is the biggest block.[5][6]

  6. 6Where it runs, and the World Championships

    What it is
    On 2 November 2026 HYROX's race finder listed 66 events from 11 November 2026 to 10 June 2027, and 36 country options across five continents.[4]
    What you do
    Examples: Europe (Dublin, Düsseldorf and Barcelona 11 to 15 Nov; Utrecht 26 to 30 Nov; London 2 to 6 Dec). North America (Denver 12 to 15 Nov; Dallas 18 to 22 Nov; Anaheim 3 to 6 Dec). Asia-Pacific (Seoul 13 to 15 Nov; Singapore 26 to 29 Nov; Melbourne 9 to 13 Dec). Middle East and Africa (Cairo 14 to 15 Nov; Dubai 27 to 28 Nov; Johannesburg 26 to 29 Nov). Latin America (Rio de Janeiro 21 to 22 Nov).[4]
    Good to know
    The PUMA HYROX World Championships are in Hong Kong, 10 to 13 June 2027.[4] Each race class at every qualifying event offers one qualification spot. Racers aged 60 and over qualify through Open.[2]

  7. 7India is one market among many

    What it is
    The finder lists IndusInd Bank HYROX Gujarat (Gandhinagar) on 26 to 28 November 2026 and HYROX Noida on 15 to 17 January 2027.[4]
    What you do
    The Gujarat page calls it HYROX's debut in Gujarat, after sell-out events in Delhi and Mumbai.[7]
    Good to know
    Same format, same weights. Entry details are on each event page. I don't quote fees.

A sample session to taste HYROX

Sample only. Not a prescription. Adjust it to your body, your health and your history.

  • Warm-up, 10 minutes: easy jog, hips, shoulders.
  • Round 1: 1 km easy run, then SkiErg 500 m.
  • Round 2: 1 km easy run, then farmers carry 100 m, light.
  • Round 3: 1 km easy run, then 20 wall balls, light ball.
  • Walk 5 minutes. Note which station felt hardest.
  • Stop when form breaks. This is a taste of the order, not a test.

Before you start

General information, not medical advice. ACSM's screening model says: if you are inactive, have no symptoms and no known heart, metabolic or kidney disease, you can start light to moderate exercise without medical clearance and build up gradually. If you have one of those conditions, or symptoms such as chest pain, unusual breathlessness or dizziness, get medical clearance first.[8] Race-style work is hard: heavy lifts, jumps, burpees, sprints and carries. Build up to it over weeks. Don't test a maximum lift alone or without a spotter or a coach watching. Sled work, burpee broad jumps and wall balls are hard on knees, back and shoulders. Build up over weeks. Ask a doctor first if you have a heart, blood-pressure or joint condition.
