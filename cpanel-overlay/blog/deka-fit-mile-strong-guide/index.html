DEKA FIT, MILE and STRONG: the formats, zones and rules

The 10 DEKA zones, weights, penalties and age groups from the official rulebook, with third-party 2026 leaderboard times and listed event dates.

By Susmit Jalan, coach at Online Fitness Worldwide. Updated 20 November 2026

DEKA is a Spartan fitness event of 10 zones completed in a fixed order.[1][3] Spartan lists STRONG as 10 zones with no running, MILE as 10 zones with 1 mile of running, FIT as 10 zones with 5K of running, Ultra as 50 zones with 25K of running, and ATLAS as 10 heavy zones with a 30-minute time cap.[2]

The DEKA Rules of Competition (v20261015) state that in MILE each zone is preceded by a 160 m run or walk (1,600 m in total) and in FIT by a 500 m run or walk (5,000 m in total). DEKA FIT Ultra is the FIT course completed 5 times (25K, 50 zones) with an 8-hour cut-off.[3]

The rulebook carries two standards charts: one in effect for the 2026 World Championship, and one taking effect on 16 October 2026 that does not apply to that championship. The figures below are from the World Championship chart. In that chart the female 14+ / male 65+ zone 1 load is printed as 33/35 lb and 15 kg.[3]

This page is independent and is not an official Spartan or DEKA publication. Rulebook and event pages were read on the dates shown in the sources.

  1. 1Zones 1 to 3

    In the sources
    Zone 1: 30 alternating reverse lunges with a RAM or dumbbell, 25 kg (55 lb) for male 14+. Zone 2: 500 m row. Zone 3: 20 box step-overs or jump-overs on a 60 cm (24 in) box.[3]

  2. 2Zones 4 to 6

    In the sources
    Zone 4: 25 med-ball sit-ups (sit-up throws in FIT, to a target centred 1.37 m up), 9 kg for male 14+ and 6 kg for female 14+. Zone 5: 500 m ski. Zone 6: 100 m farmer's carry, 27.5 kg and 17.5 kg per hand; a maze course in FIT and 5 × 10 m down-and-back in STRONG and MILE.[3]

  3. 3Zones 7 to 10

    In the sources
    Zone 7: 25 calories on an air bike. Zone 8: 20 dead-ball overs a 1.22 m structure, 27.5 kg and 17.5 kg. Zone 9: 100 m magnetic sled push/pull at a set resistance level (Xebex: 8 for male 14+, 7 for female 14+). Zone 10: 20 weighted burpees, 20 kg RAM or 2 × 10 kg (male 14+), 10 kg RAM or 2 × 5 kg (female 14+).[3]

  4. 4Penalties and unofficial finishes

    In the sources
    Completing less than 80% of the work in a zone, or of the required running, makes an unofficial finisher whose result is not submitted to the global leaderboard. Listed penalties include 10 seconds per missed rep in zones 1, 3 and 4, 20 seconds in zones 8 and 10, 1:30 per missed sled rep, 30 seconds for dropping the zone 6 weights in FIT, and 1 minute for each zone done out of order.[3]

  5. 5Categories and age groups

    In the sources
    FIT individual Elite registrations are eligible for podium prize money and Age Group registrations for podium awards. Leaderboard age is age on 30 September of the season. Individual age groups: Youth 10–11 and 12–13, 14–17, 18–24, five-year bands from 25–29 to 65–69, and 70+. Youth 10–13 have lower standards. FIT Ruck uses a 30 lb (male) or 20 lb (female) ruck.[3]

  6. 6Equipment rules

    In the sources
    The rulebook lists gloves and weightlifting belts as allowed, and lifting straps and gymnastic/CrossFit-style hand grips as not allowed.[3]

Figures from official and third-party sources

A third-party analysis of 14,580 ranked results on the 2026 DEKA global leaderboard (to 13 September 2026) reported median DEKA FIT times of 48:20 for men and 54:32 for women, fastest 2026 times of 29:35 and 31:29, and 84% of men and 70% of women finishing under an hour. It states that DEKA publishes no average finish time.[7]

Spartan lists the 2026 DEKA World Championship in Fort Worth, Texas, on 10–13 December 2026: invite-only FIT (Thursday 10), MILE (Friday 11) and STRONG (Saturday 12) championships, and public STRONG races on 12 and 13 December.[4]

Event pages read for this update list DEKA FIT in Denver (3–4 April 2027), Sacramento (1–2 May 2027) and Anaheim (1 May 2027),[5] and DEKA events in Spain from Tenerife (21–22 November 2026) to Alicante (30–31 October 2027).[6]

What the rules and guidelines state on safety

ACSM's 2015 screening model bases pre-exercise medical clearance on current activity, signs or symptoms and/or known cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease, and desired intensity.[8]
