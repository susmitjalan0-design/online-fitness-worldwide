Circuit training vs strength training: what the research shows

What studies report when the same kind of resistance exercise is done as a circuit with short rests or as traditional sets with longer rests.

By Susmit Jalan, strength and conditioning coach at Online Fitness Worldwide. Updated 21 November 2026

Meta-analyses report that resistance circuit training improves both strength and aerobic fitness. A 2021 meta-analysis of 45 studies found increases in muscle mass (1.9%) and maximal oxygen uptake (6.3%), a decrease in fat mass (4.3%), and increased upper- and lower-body strength.[2]

Head-to-head trials report mixed results. In 33 resistance-trained men, 8 weeks of high-resistance circuits with about 35 seconds between sets and traditional sets with 3 minutes' rest gave equal gains in one-repetition maximum and lean mass; body fat fell only in the circuit group and cycling peak power rose only in the traditional group.[3] In a separate 8-week trial, 3-minute rests produced greater strength and muscle thickness gains than 1-minute rests.[4]

A 2026 ACSM position stand reports that voluntary strength was enhanced by heavier loads, 2 to 3 sets and 2 or more sessions a week, and that set structure did not consistently affect outcomes.[5]

    In the sources
    Equal 1RM gains with high-resistance circuits and traditional sets in trained men;[3] greater gains with 3-minute than 1-minute rests in another trial.[4]

  2. 2Aerobic fitness

    In the sources
    Circuit training increased maximal oxygen uptake in two meta-analyses.[1][2]

  3. 3Fat mass

    In the sources
    Decreased 4.3% with circuit training; low-to-moderate intensities and short rests increased the change.[2]

  4. 4Muscle size

    In the sources
    Muscle mass rose 1.9% with circuits;[2] lean mass rose equally with circuit and traditional training in one trial.[3]

Protocols the studies used

Both groups in the head-to-head trial did 3 to 6 sets of 6 exercises at a 6-repetition maximum, 3 times a week; only the rest between sets differed.[3] The rest-interval trial used 3 sets of 8 to 12 repetitions of 7 exercises, 3 times a week, in young resistance-trained men.[4]

A 2017 meta-analysis associated large aerobic gains with about 14 to 30 circuit sessions over 6 to 12 weeks, at 60 to 90% of one-repetition maximum, and large bench-press gains with 30 to 60%; its authors note lower baseline fitness may explain the lighter optimal loads.[1]

Frequently asked questions

What is resistance circuit training?

Resistance exercises done one after another with short rests, as in the studies cited here; traditional strength training uses longer rests between sets.[2][3]

Does circuit training build strength?

A 2021 meta-analysis of 45 studies found increased upper- and lower-body strength with circuit training.[2] In trained men, high-resistance circuits and traditional sets gave equal 1RM gains over 8 weeks.[3]

Does rest between sets affect strength gains?

In one 8-week trial, 3-minute rests produced greater strength and muscle gains than 1-minute rests.[4] The 2026 ACSM position stand reports that set structure did not consistently affect outcomes.[5]

Which improved aerobic fitness?

Circuit training increased maximal oxygen uptake in two meta-analyses.[1][2] In the head-to-head trial, shuttle-run performance improved with both circuit and traditional training.[3]
