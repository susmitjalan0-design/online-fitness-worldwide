HYROX for beginners: what the data and research show

HYROX race splits and standards, and what WHO, ACSM and meta-analyses report on activity, strength progression, running increases and tapering.

By Susmit Jalan, coach at Online Fitness Worldwide. Updated 20 November 2026

A HYROX race is a 1 km run followed by one workout station, eight times, in this order: SkiErg 1,000 m, sled push 50 m, sled pull 50 m, burpee broad jumps 80 m, row 1,000 m, farmers carry 200 m, sandbag lunges 100 m and 100 wall balls.[1][2]

In a simulated Open race in HYROX's 2025 Sports Science Report, 11 recreational athletes had a median time of 86.2 minutes: 51.2 minutes running and 32.8 minutes on the stations. 70.5% of the race was at a heart rate classed as hard or very hard.[3]

RoxTimes, a third party, lists typical Open splits (men / women) including runs (8 km) 41:46 / 46:44, sled pull 4:57 / 5:46, burpee broad jumps 5:38 / 6:45, wall balls 7:02 / 6:43 and Roxzone transitions 7:22 / 7:56.[4]

HYROX's race finder, read for this update, carried 70 event listings.[10] This page is independent and is not an official HYROX plan.

Doses and protocols the studies used

WHO's 2020 guidelines state that adults should do 150 to 300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity (or 75 to 150 minutes vigorous) a week, and muscle-strengthening activities involving all major muscle groups on 2 or more days a week.[5]

ACSM's progression-models position stand recommends 8–12 repetition-maximum loads and 2–3 days a week for novices, and a 2–10% load increase when 1–2 repetitions beyond the target are possible.[6]

A meta-analysis of 43 studies found that concurrent aerobic and strength training did not significantly reduce maximal strength (SMD −0.06) or hypertrophy (−0.01) compared with strength training alone, but reduced explosive strength (−0.28), more so when both were done in the same session.[7]

In a cohort of 874 novice runners, those who increased weekly running distance by more than 30% had a higher rate of distance-related injuries than those increasing by less than 10% (hazard ratio 1.59, 95% CI 0.96 to 2.66, P = .07). The authors call the study exploratory.[8]

A meta-analysis of 27 tapering studies in competitive athletes found the largest performance effect with a 2-week taper in which training volume was reduced by 41–60% without changing intensity or frequency.[9]

What the guidelines and sources state on safety

ACSM's 2015 screening model bases pre-exercise medical clearance on current activity, signs or symptoms and/or known cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease, and desired intensity.[11]

This page reports what the cited sources found and state. It gives no advice and recommends no course of action. It is not medical advice.
