## Books (24)

### Sports nutrition and fueling (priority gap)

#### 1. NSCA's Guide to Sport and Exercise Nutrition
- **Author:** NSCA / Bill Campbell (eds.)
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=yo7xDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Anchor client fueling talk to energy, protein, fluid and timing — not supplement stacks.
  2. Match carbohydrate availability to session demand (hybrid race days vs easy strength days).
  3. Keep scope educational for online coaching; refer medical nutrition questions to qualified practitioners.

#### 2. Practical Applications in Sports Nutrition
- **Author:** Heather Hedrick Fink, Alan E. Mikesky
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=LkDkEAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Use assessment → plan → monitor loops clients can run between live calls.
  2. Translate lab-style guidance into grocery-level habits for a worldwide audience.
  3. Separate performance fueling from clinical dietetics so the brand stays inside coaching scope.

#### 3. Practical Sports Nutrition
- **Author:** Louise Burke
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=ET5GHcVBHqcC
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Periodise nutrition with the training week the same way you periodise load.
  2. Race-week and race-day plans beat vague "eat clean" advice for HYROX / DEKA / ATHX.
  3. Evidence over influencer protocols when clients ask about gels, caffeine or recovery shakes.

#### 4. Advanced Sports Nutrition (3rd ed.)
- **Author:** Dan Benardot
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=qGnyDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Within-day energy balance matters as much as weekly averages for hard-training clients.
  2. Under-fueling shows up as stalled strength, poor station times and nagging niggles.
  3. Give concrete meal-timing windows around live sessions and race stations.

#### 5. Endurance Sports Nutrition (3rd ed.)
- **Author:** Suzanne Girard Eberle
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=PcBNAQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Hybrid races still need endurance fueling logic for the run legs between stations.
  2. Practise gut tolerance in training; never debut a new fuel on race day.
  3. Hydration and sodium plans should fit climate and sweat rate, not one global template.

#### 6. Nutrition for Sport, Exercise, and Health
- **Author:** Marie Spano, Laura Kruskall, D. Travis Thomas
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=r4_iEAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Teach macronutrient roles in plain coach language for worldwide clients.
  2. Health-first framing (sleep, food quality, consistency) before optimisation tweaks.
  3. Useful backbone for blog "best fueling for X" posts without inventing results.

#### 7. ROAR
- **Author:** Stacy T. Sims, Selene Yeager
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=B2lYCwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Female physiology changes fueling, heat tolerance and recovery — programme and advise accordingly.
  2. Do not copy male templates for women clients in hybrid-race prep.
  3. Pair with Hybrid Race Coach and Physio when writing women-specific plans or content.

### Consultation selling and discovery calls

#### 8. SPIN Selling
- **Author:** Neil Rackham
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=0nM1DgAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Structure consultations as Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff questions.
  2. Let the prospect name the cost of staying stuck before you describe Iron Hour / Lean Reset / Return / Open Floor.
  3. Draft consult scripts; Susmit still agrees fees directly — never publish prices.

#### 9. The Challenger Sale
- **Author:** Matthew Dixon, Brent Adamson
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=0dRtsEpH5sgC
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Teach a useful insight in the consult (e.g. why random HIIT fails hybrid races) instead of only rapport.
  2. Reframe the problem toward concurrent training and adherence, then offer the guided plan.
  3. Works for B2B wellness conversations Sydney opens and for 1:1 consult follow-ups Nurture writes.

#### 10. New Sales. Simplified.
- **Author:** Mike Weinberg
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=jJjEuX90SVoC
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Lead with a clear sales story and prospect outcome, not "we offer online coaching".
  2. Relevance → proof → ask structure fits cold email and consult openers.
  3. Protect prospecting time; acquisition still needs a daily rhythm alongside retention.

#### 11. Pre-Suasion
- **Author:** Robert Cialdini
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=GI7tDAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. The first line of an email or call primes the answer — open on their goal or race date.
  2. Unity cues (shared sport, city, coach community) beat generic flattery.
  3. Choose one relevant hook per recipient; do not stack every programme name.

### Website UX and inbound content

#### 12. Don't Make Me Think, Revisited
- **Author:** Steve Krug
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=qahpAgAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Booking and programme pages must be self-evident on mobile worldwide.
  2. One primary action per page; cut competing CTAs.
  3. Cheap usability passes before another SEO rewrite.

### Social, video and community

#### 13. Show Your Work!
- **Author:** Austin Kleon
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=UdDaCgAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Share the process (whiteboard warm-ups, station drills) not only polished before/after claims.
  2. Document daily coaching work into Social/Video drafts without inventing client results.
  3. "Show your work" fits faceless and coach-on-camera formats equally.

#### 14. This is Marketing
- **Author:** Seth Godin
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=doVbDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Serve the smallest viable worldwide audience that cares about hybrid-race coaching.
  2. Permission and trust beat interruption; align with Nurture digests and Social calendars.
  3. Make change easy and status-safe for clients returning after a long break.

#### 15. Fanocracy
- **Author:** David Meerman Scott, Reiko Scott
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=FdOQDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Turn engaged followers into advocates with generosity and access, not hard sells.
  2. Race communities (HYROX / DEKA / ATHX) are natural fan spaces for helpful content.
  3. Pair Social storytelling with Retention's adherence systems.

#### 16. Built to Belong
- **Author:** Natalie Franke
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=a0gNEAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Community over competition — useful for Open Floor and group hybrid prep cohorts.
  2. Belonging reduces drop-off; coordinate with Retention check-ins.
  3. Design shared rituals (weekly station PR shares) clients can do remotely.

#### 17. Hooked
- **Author:** Nir Eyal
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=dsz5AwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Habit loops (trigger → action → variable reward → investment) for check-in products — used ethically.
  2. Make the next session the investment that pulls clients back.
  3. Retention owns implementation; never dark-pattern clients.

### Physiotherapy, movement quality and S&C delivery

#### 18. Becoming a Supple Leopard (2nd ed.)
- **Author:** Kelly Starrett, Glen Cordoza
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=x4GQEAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Position and tissue capacity before loading race stations or heavy compounds.
  2. Give clients simple mobilisations that fit short online warm-ups.
  3. Physio reviews any public drills for safety language and scope.

#### 19. Diagnosis and Treatment of Movement Impairment Syndromes
- **Author:** Shirley Sahrmann
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=3Z1sAAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Movement-pattern language helps Physio write regressions without diagnosing individuals online.
  2. Link common desk-worker patterns to exercise selection in blog and protocols.
  3. Always include see-a-clinician disclaimers for pain.

#### 20. Athletic Body in Balance
- **Author:** Gray Cook
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=OvB6DwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Screen movement quality before piling on hybrid volume.
  2. Corrective work is a bridge, not the whole programme.
  3. Useful shared vocabulary between Physio and Hybrid Race Coach.

#### 21. Sports Rehabilitation and Injury Prevention
- **Author:** Michael Comfort, Paul Abrahamson (eds.) / Comfort & Abrahamson listing
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=6N1qal4z7sQC
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Evidence-based return-to-training progressions for clients coming back from niggles.
  2. Integrate S&C with rehab thinking instead of stretch-only handouts.
  3. Physio owns notes; Hybrid Race Coach delays race peaking until capacity returns.

#### 22. High-Performance Training for Sports
- **Author:** David Joyce, Daniel Lewindon (eds.)
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=1vN6DwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Multi-quality programmes (strength, power, energy systems) mapped to sport demands.
  2. Transfer ideas to hybrid stations without copying team-sport playbooks blindly.
  3. Complements yesterday's Joyce & Lewindon injury text with the performance side.

#### 23. Motor Learning and Performance
- **Author:** Richard A. Schmidt, Timothy D. Lee
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=Xi-oDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Cue fewer things; external cues often transfer better on camera than anatomy lectures.
  2. Practice design (blocked vs random, feedback frequency) matters for remote clients.
  3. Upgrade how Video and live sessions teach lifts and stations.

#### 24. The Coach's Guide to Teaching
- **Author:** Doug Lemov
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=ePSgEAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Check for understanding every live session; do not assume the demo landed.
  2. Break complex stations into teachable chunks then recombine.
  3. Applies to online 1:1 and small-group Open Floor delivery worldwide.

### Extra verified titles for Retention / membership / B2B (short)

#### 25. Tiny Habits
- **Author:** B. J. Fogg
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=5TmbDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Shrink the starter habit until clients cannot fail (one pass-through, one core set).
  2. Celebrate immediately to wire adherence between calls.
  3. Retention playbooks should specify the tiny version of every ask.

#### 26. The Membership Economy
- **Author:** Robbie Kellman Baxter
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=kKQDoQEACAAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Design for the forever transaction: ongoing coaching relationship, not one campaign.
  2. Super-users and clear onboarding reduce churn — hand to Retention + Nurture.
  3. Free-to-paid paths must stay honest: no invented social proof.

#### 27. Corporate Wellness Programs
- **Author:** Ronald J. Burke, Astrid M. Richardsen (eds.)
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=dMcwBQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. B2B wellness buys need organisational health framing, not only individual fat-loss copy.
  2. Sydney can use this to brief HR / benefits prospects after cold openers.
  3. Keep targeting businesses and roles, never personal health conditions.

#### 28. New Functional Training for Sports
- **Author:** Michael Boyle
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=iRmVEAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Single-leg strength, core anti-rotation and progressive power transfer to hybrid stations.
  2. Keep exercise menus compatible with Susmit's whiteboard style and short sessions.
  3. Hybrid Race Coach + Physio co-review before client PDFs ship.
