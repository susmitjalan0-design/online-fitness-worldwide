Use this document as a starting point for discussion with your coach and, when needed, that professional — not as a personal prescription.

## Why this matters

Strength work and hybrid-race training both ask a lot of your muscles and your fuel stores. Two habits make the biggest everyday difference for most athletes we coach:

1. **Enough protein each day**, spread across meals, so you can repair and adapt after lifting and station work.  
2. **Matching carbohydrate** to the day in front of you — more on hard sessions and race sims, a bit less on easy or recovery days — the same way your programme periodises load.

That “match the fuel to the session” idea shows up across practical sports-nutrition coaching texts: keep the conversation on energy, protein, fluid and timing; periodise nutrition with the training week; and treat race weeks with a plan rather than vague “eat clean” advice.

## Daily protein targets

### A simple target range

For most healthy, training adults who lift and do hybrid conditioning, widely cited sports-nutrition position stands land in a similar band:

| Source (verified) | Daily protein guidance |
|---|---|
| **ISSN Position Stand: Protein and Exercise (2017)** | About **1.4–2.0 g per kg** body weight per day is enough for most exercising people who want to build or maintain muscle. |
| **ACSM / Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics / Dietitians of Canada — Nutrition and Athletic Performance (2016)** | About **1.2–2.0 g per kg** per day to support training adaptation, repair and recovery; higher short-term intakes may be useful in hard training blocks or when calories are cut. |

**How we talk about it with clients (metric + lb):**

- Pick a working target inside **~1.4–2.0 g/kg/day** unless a qualified professional has set something else for you.  
- **Quick conversion:** body weight in lb ÷ 2.2 ≈ weight in kg.  
  - Example: **70 kg (about 154 lb)** × 1.6 g/kg ≈ **112 g protein/day**.  
  - Same person at 1.8 g/kg ≈ **126 g/day**.  
- Per meal, many athletes do well with roughly **0.25–0.4 g/kg** of high-quality protein (often landing near **20–40 g** per eating occasion), spread every few hours rather than in one giant serving.

You do not need a supplement to hit these numbers. Eggs, dairy or fortified plant milks, yogurt, fish, poultry, lean meat, tofu, tempeh, beans, lentils and protein-rich grains all count. Choose what fits your culture, budget, ethics and gut comfort.

**Women athletes:** fueling needs still scale with body size and training load. Female physiology can change heat tolerance, recovery and day-to-day energy feel across the cycle — so copy-paste “one template for everyone” plans are a poor fit. If energy, mood or performance swings hard with your cycle, bring that to your coach and consider a professional who works with female athletes.

## Carbohydrate periodisation: hard days vs easy days

Carbohydrate is the main high-intensity fuel for heavy lifts, sleds, wall balls, ski erg efforts and the run legs between stations. The 2016 ACSM / AND / Dietitians of Canada position stand frames daily carbohydrate by **training load** when you want high carbohydrate availability (enough muscle and brain fuel for quality work):

| Training load (guide) | Carbohydrate target |
|---|---|
| **Light** — low intensity or skill / technique focus | **3–5 g/kg/day** |
| **Moderate** — roughly ~1 hour of training | **5–7 g/kg/day** |
| **High** — ~1–3 hours moderate–high intensity | **6–10 g/kg/day** |
| **Very high** — extreme multi-hour days | **8–12 g/kg/day** |

On days when session quality matters less, or when the plan deliberately uses lower carbohydrate availability, intake can sit below those “high availability” targets — guided by energy needs, food preference and how you feel in training. That is the heart of **carbohydrate periodisation**: more fuel when the work is hard and race-like; less when the day is easy or recovery-focused.

### How Online Fitness Worldwide clients can apply this

| Day type | What it usually looks like | Carbohydrate approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard day** | Heavy strength + conditioning, hybrid race simulation, long brick-style session, or key interval work | Aim toward the **moderate-to-high** band for your body weight (often **~5–8+ g/kg**, fine-tuned with how long and hard the session is). Put carbs **before and after** the main session. |
| **Easy day** | Technique, mobility, light aerobic work, deload, or rest with only a short lift | Aim toward the **light-to-lower-moderate** band (often **~3–5 g/kg**). Keep protein steady; shrink the rice/pasta/bread/potato portions, not the protein. |

**Worked example — 70 kg (≈ 154 lb) athlete**

- Hard day at ~6 g/kg → ~**420 g carbohydrate**.  
- Easy day at ~4 g/kg → ~**280 g carbohydrate**.  
- Protein held near ~1.6 g/kg → ~**112 g** both days.

Your exact numbers will differ with body size, climate, sweat rate, race calendar and whether you are gaining, maintaining or cutting. The pattern matters more than perfection on day one.

**Hybrid-race note:** HYROX / DEKA / ATHX still borrow **endurance fueling logic** for the running between stations. Practise gut tolerance (what you eat and drink in long sessions) in training — never debut a new gel, drink or huge fibre load on race day.

**Within-day balance:** under-fueling on hard training days often shows up as stalled strength, slower stations and nagging niggles. Eating enough across the day — not only at dinner — helps.

## Example days (globally common foods)

These are **illustrations**, not meal plans. Portions are approximate for a ~70 kg (154 lb) athlete. Swap like-for-like foods that you already eat and tolerate.

### Hard training day (higher carbohydrate)

**Morning (pre-session focus)**  
- Bowl of oats cooked in milk or fortified plant milk, topped with a banana and a spoon of honey or maple  
- Eggs or Greek-style yogurt on the side  

**During / around a long or race-sim session (if needed)**  
- Water as your base; add a familiar sports drink or easily digested carbs you have practised (for example diluted juice, a ripe banana, or a product you already tolerate)  

**Lunch (recovery)**  
- Rice or pasta bowl with chicken, fish, tofu or beans  
- Plenty of the grain, plus vegetables you digest well  
- Fruit  

**Afternoon snack**  
- Toast or a wrap with peanut butter or hummus, or yogurt with fruit  

**Dinner**  
- Potato or rice with a palm-sized protein (fish, poultry, lean meat, tempeh)  
- Vegetables  
- Optional bread on the side if you are still hungry and the session was long  

**Evening (optional)**  
- Milk or yogurt-based snack if it helps you hit protein without upsetting sleep  

*Idea:* carbs denser around the hard session; protein at each main eating occasion.

### Easy / recovery day (lower carbohydrate, protein steady)

**Morning**  
- Eggs with vegetables and one slice of toast **or** yogurt with berries and a smaller serve of oats  

**Lunch**  
- Large salad or vegetable plate with tuna, chicken, tofu or lentils  
- A modest scoop of rice, quinoa or one wrap — not a mountain  

**Snack**  
- Cottage cheese, yogurt, edamame, or a small handful of nuts plus fruit  

**Dinner**  
- Stir-fry or grill: protein + lots of vegetables  
- Smaller starch (½ the hard-day rice/potato portion)  

**Evening**  
- Same optional protein-focused snack if needed  

*Idea:* plates still look like real meals; you mainly turn the carb dial down, not protein or produce.

## A simple plate check you can use anywhere

Between live calls, a grocery-level loop works well worldwide:

1. **Assess** yesterday: Did hard sessions feel flat? Was protein present at most meals?  
2. **Plan** today: Is this a hard or easy day on your Online Fitness Worldwide programme?  
3. **Monitor** for 1–2 weeks: session quality, hunger, sleep, digestion — then adjust portions, not every food rule at once.

**Plate sketch**

- **Hard day:** ½ plate carbohydrate foods · ¼ plate protein · ¼ plate vegetables/fruit (+ fluids)  
- **Easy day:** ¼ plate carbohydrate foods · ¼–⅓ plate protein · the rest vegetables/fruit (+ fluids)

Health basics (sleep, food quality, consistency) come before optimisation tweaks.
