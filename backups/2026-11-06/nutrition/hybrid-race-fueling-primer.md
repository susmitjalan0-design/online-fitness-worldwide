## Who this is for

Athletes and recreational competitors preparing for **hybrid fitness races** such as **HYROX**, **DEKA**, and **ATHX**: events that mix running (or similar locomotion) with heavy, repeated station work. The same fueling logic applies whether you train live online from London, Dubai, Singapore, São Paulo, or Sydney — adjust climate, travel, and food availability to **your** setting, not a single national template.

---

## 1. Training-day fueling vs race-day fueling

Sports nutrition texts consistently separate **everyday training nutrition** from **competition nutrition**. Match carbohydrate availability to the work you are asking your body to do; keep protein and fluids steady; use race week to lock in familiar foods rather than inventing a new "perfect" diet overnight.

| Context | What the day asks for | Coach-language focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Easy / recovery day** | Lower total work, technique, mobility, light strength | Normal meals; no need to "carb load"; keep protein at meals; hydrate to thirst plus climate |
| **Hard session day** (heavy stations, long brick, race-sim) | High glycogen demand + muscle repair | Extra carbohydrate around the session; protein after; practise race fuels if the session mimics race demand |
| **Race week** | Taper load, keep glycogen topped up, reduce gut risk | Familiar foods; slightly more carbohydrate as volume drops; lock caffeine/fluid plan; sleep and travel logistics |
| **Race day** | One hard effort with run + stations | Proven pre-race meal; known in-race carbs/fluids only; no experiments |

**Key idea (periodisation):** Periodise nutrition with the training week the same way you periodise load — race-week and race-day plans beat vague "eat clean" advice for hybrid events.

**Key idea (within-day balance):** Within-day energy balance matters as much as weekly averages. Under-fueling often shows up as stalled strength, slower station times, and nagging niggles — not only as a number on the scale.

## 2. A simple plate / portion method (works in any kitchen)

Use a **plate method** you can apply in a hotel breakfast buffet, a home kitchen, or a work cafeteria. Hands and plate sections travel better than gram scales when you are worldwide and busy.

### Everyday mixed plate (easy / moderate days)

- **½ plate** colourful plants (vegetables, salad, fruit)  
- **¼ plate** protein (eggs, fish, poultry, dairy, tofu, legumes, or a mix you tolerate)  
- **¼ plate** carbohydrate staple (rice, potatoes, pasta, bread, oats, tortillas, noodles — choose what you digest well)  
- **Add** a thumb of fats (olive oil, nuts, avocado, cheese) as appetite and culture allow  
- **Fluid** with meals; more in heat or after sweaty sessions  

### Hard-session / race-prep plate

Shift the plate toward **more carbohydrate** and keep protein present:

- **~⅓–½ plate** carbohydrate staple (familiar, low-surprise foods)  
- **~¼ plate** protein  
- **Remainder** easy vegetables / fruit  
- Keep fibre and very spicy or brand-new foods **lower** the day before and morning of a key session or race if your gut is sensitive  

### Hand portions (quick check between live calls)

- Protein: roughly **1 palm** per main meal (adjust for body size and hunger)  
- Carbs (hard days): roughly **1–2 cupped hands** of cooked staple at the meals around the session  
- Carbs (easy days): roughly **1 cupped hand** or skip the extra handful if appetite is low  
- Fats: roughly **1 thumb** per meal  

These are **coaching heuristics**, not clinical prescriptions. Reassess with your coach using an assess → plan → monitor loop: note energy in sessions, station quality, sleep, and gut comfort for 1–2 weeks, then adjust portions — not influencer protocols.

## 3. Protein and carbohydrate timing: hard sessions vs easy days

Anchor talk to **energy, protein, fluid, and timing** — not supplement stacks.

### Protein

- Spread protein across **2–4 meals** rather than one giant evening meal.  
- Include a protein source in the meal or snack **after** hard strength or hybrid sessions to support recovery alongside carbohydrate.  
- Easy days still need protein; you do not drop it because carbs dropped.  

### Carbohydrate

- **Match carbohydrate availability to session demand:** more around hybrid race-sims, long run legs, and dense station days; less urgency on easy strength or recovery days.  
- Prefer **familiar** carb foods you already tolerate (oats, rice, bread, potatoes, ripe fruit, yoghurt + cereal, etc.).  
- For hybrid races, remember the **run legs between stations** still use endurance fueling logic even though the event also demands strength and power.  

### Timing windows (practical, not clinical)

- **2–4 hours before** a hard session or race: a full, familiar meal with carbohydrate + some protein; moderate fat/fibre if your gut prefers that.  
- **30–90 minutes before** (if needed): a smaller, practised snack (e.g. banana + yoghurt, toast + honey, a known bar) — only if you have tested it.  
- **During** long or high-sweat sessions that mimic race length: practised fluids and, if the session is long enough that energy dips, practised carbohydrate (drink, gel, chew, or real food you have used in training).  
- **After:** carbohydrate + protein within a comfortable window when you can eat; fluid and sodium awareness if you finished very salty/sweaty.  

Exact gram-per-kilogram targets belong in a personalised plan with a qualified practitioner when needed. This primer stays at **coach-usable patterns**.

## 4. Race week (HYROX / DEKA / ATHX)

Race week is for **familiarity and glycogen**, not for a new diet.

1. **Keep foods you already know.** Do not start a new elimination diet, new high-fibre "cleanse," or new sports product this week.  
2. **As training volume tapers, keep carbohydrate intake meaningful** at meals so glycogen stays topped up — especially if you have been training hard.  
3. **Practise the exact pre-race breakfast** at least once on a hard training morning earlier in the block (see section 5).  
4. **Map logistics:** venue climate, travel time, what you can carry, water stations vs BYO, heat/humidity vs cold hall. Hydration and sodium plans should fit **climate and your sweat pattern**, not one global template.  
5. **Women athletes:** do not copy a male teammate's template by default. Female physiology can change fueling needs, heat tolerance, and recovery across the cycle and life stages — programme and advise accordingly; pair questions with your Hybrid Race Coach (and Physio if pain or capacity is the issue).  
6. **Sleep, caffeine cut-off, and travel meals** matter as much as the "race gel." Within-day under-fueling on travel days is a common trap.

## 5. Race day and in-race fueling & hydration

### Pre-race meal

- Eat the **same meal** you practised.  
- Emphasise carbohydrate, include some protein, keep fat and fibre at the level **you** tolerate.  
- Finish the main meal early enough that you are not still digesting hard at the start line (many athletes use a 2–4 hour gap; your practised gap wins).  
- Optional small top-up only if practised.  

### In-race (between stations / on run legs)

Hybrid races are usually shorter than marathons, but they are still **high intensity with limited gut blood flow**. Keep in-race fueling **simple and practised**:

- **Fluids:** sip to plan based on heat, sweat, and how you felt in race-sims — not a brand-new "must drink X bottles" rule.  
- **Carbohydrate during the race:** only if you need it for *your* event length and intensity, and only products/foods you have used in training. Many athletes in shorter hybrid formats rely mainly on a strong pre-race meal plus fluids; longer or hotter races may need mid-event carbs.  
- **Sodium:** if you are a heavy salty sweater or racing in heat, use the **same** fluid/electrolyte approach you practised — do not invent a high-sodium protocol on the day.  
- **Never debut** a new gel, drink, gum, or solid on race day.  

### After the finish

- Start with fluids; then a normal meal with carbohydrate + protein when appetite returns.  
- Celebration food is fine; the priority is restoring fuel and fluid, not punishment or restriction.

## 6. What to practise in training (never debut on race day)

Borrow endurance-sport discipline even though your sport is hybrid:

| Practise in training | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Gut tolerance** of your race breakfast and any in-race carb/fluid | GI distress is a performance limiter; training is the lab |
| **Caffeine** dose and timing (coffee, tea, or practised alternative) | Stimulant response is individual; race day is the wrong first trial |
| **Fluid volumes** in heat vs indoor cool venues | Sweat rate and comfort vary by climate and kit |
| **Carry method** (belt, handheld, pockets) with station work | Hands are busy on sleds, wall balls, ski erg, etc. |
| **Travel / buffet versions** of your plate method | Worldwide athletes race away from home kitchens |

**Rule:** If you have not used it in a hard brick or race-sim, it does not go in the race bag.

Evidence over influencer protocols when clients ask about gels, caffeine, or recovery shakes: use what you have tested; refer supplement or medical questions to a qualified professional.

## 7. Health-first backbone (before optimisation)

Before micro-optimising gels:

1. Consistent meals most days  
2. Enough total energy for your training load (within-day and across the week)  
3. Sleep and stress management  
4. Food quality you can sustain in your culture and budget  
5. Referral out when red flags appear (persistent fatigue, menstrual disruption, fixation on food rules, unexplained weight change, pain that needs clinical care)

Health-first framing beats optimisation tweaks for long-term hybrid-race progress.
