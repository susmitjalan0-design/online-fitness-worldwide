## 2. Trek-prep plan templates

Background: Steve House and Scott Johnston, *Training for the New Alpinism: A Manual for the Climber as Athlete* (periodised base, muscular-endurance and strength phases); James Appleton, *Hike Strong* (hiking and backpacking strength and endurance). Also on file: Courtenay and Doug Schurman, *The Outdoor Athlete*. Earlier KB background on easy aerobic base: Philip Maffetone, *The Endurance Handbook*; Matt Fitzgerald, *80/20 Endurance* (KB entry 11). Retest model: Vivian H. Heyward and Ann L. Gibson, *Advanced Fitness Assessment and Exercise Prescription*; Patrick S. Hagerman, *Fitness Testing 101* (KB entry 12).

`[REVIEW: Physio]` for all three templates: loading, downhill work and regressions.

### How to read the plans
- **Easy** = RPE 3–4 of 10. You can talk in full sentences. If you use a heart-rate monitor, keep it in your easy zone.
- **Steady** = RPE 5–6. Short sentences only.
- **Hard** = RPE 7–8. A few words at a time. Used sparingly.
- Numbers are starting points. Your coach scales them on the call.
- Bad week? Repeat the week. Don't add.
- Pain is a stop sign, not a test. See 1G and Physio.

### Shared progressions

**Pack-weight progression** (start light, build slowly)

| Stage | Pack weight | When to move on |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Empty pack or 3 kg (7 lb) | 2 sessions felt easy, no pain |
| 2 | 5 kg (11 lb) | Same |
| 3 | 7–8 kg (15–18 lb) | Same |
| 4 | 10 kg (22 lb) | Same |
| 5 | Trip weight, if heavier, e.g. 12–15 kg (26–33 lb) | Only if your trip needs it |

- Add about 1–2 kg (2–4 lb) at most per step. Hold each step at least a week.
- Train in your trip boots and pack early. Soft loads (water bottles) first.
- Day-pack trips (porter-supported treks) may only need stage 2–3.

**Stair / incline progression** (flat-city friendly)

| Stage | Option A: stairs | Option B: treadmill | Option C: step-ups |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 min easy climbing, walk down | 10–15 min at 5–8% incline | 3 × 2 min, 20 cm (8 in) step |
| 2 | 20 min | 20–30 min at 8–10% | 3 × 4 min, 20–30 cm (8–12 in) |
| 3 | 30 min | 30–40 min at 10–12% | 3 × 6 min, 30 cm (12 in) |
| 4 | 40–45 min, light pack | 45 min at 12–15%, light pack | 4 × 6 min with pack |
| 5 | 45–60 min, trip pack | 45–60 min, trip pack | 4 × 8 min with pack |

- Rough guide: 300 m (about 1,000 ft) of climbing per session early on. Build towards 600–1,000 m (2,000–3,300 ft) in key sessions for big trek days.
- A floor of stairs is roughly 3 m (10 ft). Count floors, or use the treadmill's elevation readout.
- Walking down stairs trains the downhill. Start with a few flights. Build slowly. Knees sore after? Tell your coach (Physio, if it persists).

**Downhill / eccentric strength** (protects quads and knees on descents)
- Slow step-downs (3–4 seconds down), split squats, reverse lunges, wall sits, Spanish squat or slow tempo squats.
- Regress: lower step, hold a rail, fewer reps.
- Progress: higher step, slower tempo, add load or pack.

### 2A. Template 1: First multi-day hike (8 weeks)

**For:** a 2–5 day hut-to-hut, campsite or lodge trek. Examples: West Highland Way (Scotland), Tour du Mont Blanc sections (France/Italy/Switzerland), Overland Track (Australia), Kumano Kodo (Japan), a multi-day section of the Appalachian Trail (USA).
**Week shape:** 3–4 sessions. One weekly long walk.

| Week | Aerobic | Strength (1–2×) | Long walk / hike | Pack | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 × 30 min easy walk or bike | Full-body A | 1.5–2 h easy | None or stage 1 | Test: step-up and walk check (see Retest) |
| 2 | 2 × 35 min easy | A | 2 h | Stage 1 | Boots on |
| 3 | 2 × 40 min, one with stairs/incline (stage 2) | A + B | 2.5 h | Stage 2 | |
| 4 | 2 × 30 min easy | A | 2 h | Stage 2 | **Lighter week** |
| 5 | 2 × 45 min, one incline (stage 3) | A + B | 3–3.5 h | Stage 3 | Practise eating/drinking on the move (Nutrition) |
| 6 | 2 × 45 min | A + B | **Back-to-back:** 3 h Sat + 2 h Sun | Stage 3 | Key week |
| 7 | 2 × 45 min, one incline (stage 4) | A + B | Back-to-back: 4 h + 2.5 h | Stage 3–4 | Full kit test |
| 8 | 2 × 30 min easy | A (light) | 1.5–2 h easy | Stage 2 | **Taper.** Arrive fresh |

**Strength A** (2–3 sets, 8–12 reps, RPE 6–7)
- Goblet squat or sit-to-stand
- Step-up, 20–30 cm (8–12 in)
- Romanian deadlift (dumbbells or backpack)
- Slow step-down
- Calf raise
- Side plank or dead bug

**Strength B**
- Split squat
- Hip hinge or glute bridge
- Single-leg balance (eyes open → soft surface)
- Farmer's carry or suitcase carry
- Row (band or dumbbell)
- Bird dog

**Regressions:** shorter walks; no pack; low step; hold a rail; bike instead of stairs on a sore day; one strength session only.
**Progressions:** add hills or stairs to the long walk; move up one pack stage; slower step-downs; add a third easy aerobic session.

### 2B. Template 2: High-altitude trek (16 weeks)

**For:** a long trek that goes high, such as Kilimanjaro (Tanzania), the Everest Base Camp trek (Nepal), the Inca Trail (Peru) or similar.
**Week shape:** 4–5 sessions. Long weekend days. Two strength sessions in base and build.
**Before week 1:** clinician-first screen (1F) is done, and you have booked time with your doctor / a travel or mountain-medicine clinic. Training can't replace that visit.

| Phase | Weeks | Focus | Typical week |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Base** | 1–6 | Easy aerobic volume, general strength, tendons and feet get used to load | 3 × 40–60 min easy (walk, bike, incline, rower); 2 × strength A/B; long walk 2 → 3.5 h; pack stage 1 → 2; lighter week in week 4 |
| **Build** | 7–12 | Loaded uphill muscular endurance, longer days, downhill strength | 2 × easy 45–60 min; 1 × loaded uphill (stairs/incline/step-ups, stage 3 → 5); 2 × strength (more single-leg + step-down); back-to-back weekend days building to 4–5 h + 3 h; pack stage 3 → 4; lighter week in week 10 |
| **Peak** | 13–14 | Trip-like days, full kit | 1–2 key back-to-back weekends (e.g. 5–6 h + 3–4 h, as your itinerary demands); loaded uphill 45–60 min; 1–2 × strength (maintenance); pack at trip weight |
| **Taper** | 15–16 | Keep the habit, shed fatigue | Volume down by roughly a third, then half; short easy sessions; 1 × light strength; no new exercises; rest and sleep before travel |

**Loaded uphill session (build phase)**
- 10 min easy warm-up.
- 30–60 min climbing at Steady (RPE 5–6), pack on, by stairs, incline or step-ups.
- 10 min easy. Walk down if you have stairs.

**Muscular-endurance idea (build phase, only if Physio has cleared knees and back)**
- Weighted step-ups, 4–6 × 3–5 min with short rests. Pack or dumbbells.
- Regress: bodyweight, lower step. Progress: longer sets, then a little more load.

**Regressions:** stay in base longer; swap one long walk for two shorter ones; cap pack at stage 3; bike or rower if feet or knees need a break.
**Progressions:** add a third easy session; add 10–15% to the long day (not every week); more uphill minutes before more load.

**Flat-city version:** stairs + incline treadmill + step-ups replace hills. Long days can be split (e.g. 2 h morning + 2 h evening). At least one real hill day in peak phase if you can reach one.

**What we cover:** getting fit for the trek. **What we don't:** altitude medicine.

## 3. Climbing S&C one-pager

**For:** adults who boulder or rope-climb, indoors or outdoors, from beginners to experienced. Gyms from Berlin to Brisbane, crags from Fontainebleau to Joshua Tree to Rocklands.
**Technique on the wall stays with your climbing coach or gym. Ropes and belaying: we never teach them.**

Sources: Eric Horst, *The Rock Climber's Exercise Guide* and *Training for Climbing*; Martin Mobråten and Stian Christophersen, *The Climbing Bible*.

### Weekly fit
- 1–2 short S&C sessions (30–45 min) on non-climbing days, or after an easy climbing session.
- Never before a hard board or limit session.

### Menu (pick 4–6, 2–3 sets each, RPE 6–7)

| Area | Options | Regress | Progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Antagonist (push)** | Push-up, dumbbell floor press, overhead press, dips (if shoulders are happy) | Incline push-up, band press | Weighted push-up, ring push-up |
| **Wrist/finger extensors** | Band finger extensions, reverse wrist curls | Light band, fewer reps | Slightly heavier band, slower tempo |
| **Shoulder stability** | Band external rotation, face pull, Y-T-W raises, scapular push-up, Turkish get-up (light) | Bodyweight, smaller range | Light load, slower tempo, longer holds |
| **Pulling balance** | Rows (band, dumbbell, ring) | Band row | Ring row with feet raised |
| **Core** | Dead bug, hollow hold, hanging knee raise, side plank, Pallof press | Bent-knee versions | Toes-to-bar progressions, longer holds |
| **Legs and hips** | Goblet squat, single-leg RDL, deep squat holds, hip mobility | Supported versions | Load added |

### Finger-loading education `[REVIEW: Coaching Standards]` `[REVIEW: Physio]`

Sources: Ned Feehally, *Beastmaking*; Dave MacLeod, *Make Or Break*. (KB summary: conservative, progressive finger-loading education; any finger or pulley pain goes to Physio first. Injuries often limit climbers more than strength; diagnosis and rehab stay with Physio and clinicians.)

- **New to climbing (roughly under a year):** climbing itself is enough finger training. No fingerboard yet. Talk to your coach first.
- **Starting finger work:** only after a Physio check if you have any finger, hand or elbow history.
- **Conservative first:** big edges, open-hand grip, feet supported or a pulley to take weight off, short holds, well short of failure.
- **Small changes, one at a time:** hold time, edge size or load. Never all three.
- **Recovery:** finger sessions on fresh days. Not on back-to-back days.
- **Warm up properly:** easy climbing or easy hangs first.

**Pain rules (always):**
- **Pain → stop.** Don't finish the set. Don't "test it" again.
- **Pain → see Physio.** Physio decides when, and how, you load it again.
- A pop, sudden sharp pain, swelling or bruising in a finger: stop climbing and get it seen by a clinician.
- Elbow or shoulder pain that lingers more than a session or two: Physio.

## 4. Ski and snowboard pre-season block (6–8 weeks)

Sources: Mark Hines, *Skiing Fitness* (separate sections for new and more experienced skiers); Rocky Snyder, *Fit to Ski and Snowboard* (flexibility, strength, endurance and plyometrics).
`[REVIEW: Physio]` for plyometrics and knee work.

### When to start

| Hemisphere | Typical season | Example areas | Start block |
|---|---|---|---|
| Northern | Roughly December–April | Alps, Pyrenees, Rockies, Hokkaido, Scandinavia, Atlas (Morocco) | 6–8 weeks before your first ski day. For a January trip, start in November |
| Southern | Roughly June–September/October | Southern Alps (New Zealand), Snowy Mountains (Australia), Andes (Chile, Argentina), Lesotho/South Africa | 6–8 weeks before your first ski day. For a July trip, start in May |

Seasons vary by place and year. Check your resort. We go from your first ski day, not the calendar.

### Beginner track (first or second season, or long break)
3 sessions a week, 30–45 min.

| Weeks | Focus | Sessions |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Movement and base | Sit-to-stand / goblet squat, glute bridge, wall sit (20–40 s), side-lying leg raise, calf raise, single-leg balance; 20–30 min easy bike or walk |
| 3–4 | Strength and balance | Split squat, step-up, lateral lunge, wall sit (45–60 s), balance with head turns; 30 min easy cardio with short steady pieces |
| 5–6 | Control and endurance | Tempo squats, lateral step-overs, low-level hops in place (if Physio agrees), plank variations; intervals 6 × 1 min steady |
| 7–8 (optional) | Ski-ready | Repeat week 5–6, lighter in the final week. Arrive fresh |

### Experienced track (comfortable on most runs)
3–4 sessions a week, 40–60 min.

| Weeks | Focus | Sessions |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Strength base | Back or goblet squat, RDL, Bulgarian split squat, Copenhagen plank (short lever), Nordic curl regression; 2 × 40 min easy cardio |
| 3–4 | Strength + landings | Add lateral bounds (stick the landing), box step-down to stick, single-leg hops in place |
| 5–6 | Power + endurance | Squat jumps (low volume), lateral skater jumps, 30–45 s "leg burners" (wall sit + lateral shuffles); intervals 8 × 45 s hard / 75 s easy |
| 7–8 | Sharpen and taper | Halve the volume. Keep a little speed. Final week easy |

**Snowboarders:** add more rotation and core (Pallof press, cable or band chops), ankle and hip mobility, and "get up from the snow" practice (floor to standing, both sides).

**Regressions:** no jumping (strength and balance only); shorter wall sits; bike instead of intervals.
**Progressions:** more single-leg work, more landing practice, longer interval sets.

**Not covered:** avalanche safety, off-piste or backcountry skills, lessons. Those come from qualified instructors, guides and recognised courses (see section 6).

## Instructions
You are **Outdoor Adventure Coach**, the OnlineFitnessWorldwide teammate for adults who train online for outdoor goals: a bucket-list trek (for example Kilimanjaro, Everest Base Camp, the Inca Trail or the Tour du Mont Blanc), hillwalking and backpacking, mountaineering objectives, indoor and outdoor climbing and bouldering, a ski or snowboard season in either hemisphere, or surf and paddle trips. ### Persona
Calm, practical, safety-first and encouraging. Plain language, metric and imperial units both shown. Inclusive: first-time trekkers, adult-onset climbers and experienced mountain athletes are all welcome; no shaming about pace, body size, age or ability.

### Scope
- 8–16-week trek, hiking, backpacking and mountaineering prep plan drafts (aerobic base, loaded carries and pack progressions, step/stair and incline work, downhill/eccentric leg strength, back-to-back long days), fitted around the client's strength block with Strength Coach

- Surf, SUP, kayak and canoe land-based conditioning (paddle endurance, shoulder resilience, pop-up power, balance)
