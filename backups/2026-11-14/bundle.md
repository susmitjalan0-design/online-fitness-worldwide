## Books (28)

### Tendon health, blood-flow-restriction and load-managed rehab (education; Physio reviews)
#### 1. Tendinopathy: From Basic Science to Clinical Management
- **Author:** Kentaro Onishi, Michael Fredericson, Jason L. Dragoo (eds.)
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=rLsyEAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Clinic guide covering tendon physiology, pathophysiology, biomechanics and mechanotransduction.
  2. Region-by-region chapters (rotator cuff to wrist, groin and gluteal to foot and ankle) on presentation, examination, imaging and treatment options.
  3. Background for Physio's red-flag and referral notes; coaches don't diagnose tendon pain, they adjust load and refer.
#### 2. Therapeutic Exercise for Musculoskeletal Injuries (4th ed.)
- **Author:** Peggy A. Houglum
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=qPF6DwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Rehab textbook explaining what happens in the body, why techniques work and when to use them along the rehab timeline.
  2. Online video demonstrates the harder techniques, useful as a model for our own demo clips.
  3. Helps coaches understand where clinician-led rehab ends and coach-led return to training starts.
#### 3. Clinical Application and Impact of Blood-Flow-Restriction Training
- **Author:** Alexander Franz, Michael Behringer, Luke Hughes (eds.)
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=pP7lEAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Research collection on BFR training for muscle mass and function in clinical and ageing populations.
  2. Frames BFR as a way to get training stimulus at low loads when heavy loading isn't possible.
  3. Specialist method: content mentions it as education only; any use is under qualified supervision with screening, never self-prescribed from a post.
#### 4. Rock Solid Resilience: An Evidence-Based Guide to Preventing Injury, Optimizing Strength, and Enhancing Performance
- **Author:** Dean Somerset, Dan Pope
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=tuErEQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Build resistance training around how you move, your training stage and goals to reduce injury risk and keep lifting for life.
  2. Rejects blanket 'if it hurts, don't do it'; teaches when to modify rather than stop or push through.
  3. Plain-language source for client education on training around niggles (with Physio sign-off).

### Grip, carries and strongman-style implement training for race stations
#### 5. Strongman Training for Strength and Performance
- **Author:** Christopher Slater, Heidi Campo
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=Gd28EQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Accessible guide to using strongman implements and exercises for non-competitors.
  2. Shows how to add carries, sled-type and odd-object work to normal programmes for performance and variety.
  3. Direct transfer to farmers carry, sandbag lunge and sled stations in hybrid races.
#### 6. Get a Grip: A Practical Primer on Grip Strength and Endurance Training... and More
- **Author:** Scott Burr
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=ePlPzAEACAAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. From-the-ground-up primer on grip strength and grip endurance.
  2. Uses cheap, household or DIY equipment, a fit for home-based online clients.
  3. Grip endurance is often the limiter on carries, sled ropes and long ski/row pulls.
#### 7. Tactical Strongman: The Complete Guide
- **Author:** Adam benShea, Josh Bryant
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=AlmOzQEACAAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Strongman-based programming that combines limit strength, hypertrophy and explosive strength.
  2. Idea bank for implement progressions; treat the blurb's results claims as the authors' marketing.
  3. Use the methods, not the hype, in our own content.

### On-camera presenting, speaking voice and storytelling
#### 8. Talk Like TED: The 9 Public Speaking Secrets of the World's Top Minds
- **Author:** Carmine Gallo
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=upebAgAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Breaks down techniques top TED speakers use to make talks engaging, novel and memorable.
  2. Applies to any presentation, and helps with nerves about speaking.
  3. Template for YouTube intros, webinar openings and live-class welcomes.

- **Author:** Chris Anderson
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=AWspCgAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Insider guide from TED's head on building talks that change minds.

  3. Useful for long-form explainers and founder talks for Susmit.

- **Author:** Michael Port
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=6vGOCgAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Actor-turned-speaker frames every interaction as a performance and shows how to manage nerves.

  3. Helps on consult calls and live sessions without sounding scripted.

- **Author:** Orlando R. Barone, Cari M. Tellis
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=lXtNDAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Combines voice science with practical presentation skills.
  2. Covers positioning, gesture and paraverbals (pace, pitch, pause).
  3. Helps coaches cue clearly over laggy video calls and record clean voiceovers.
#### 13. Storyworthy: Engage, Teach, Persuade, and Change Your Life through the Power of Storytelling
- **Author:** Matthew Dicks
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=TihZDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Moth champion's straightforward method for building, telling and polishing stories.
  2. Anyone can learn it; everyday moments make the best material.
  3. Source for founder and coach stories (never invented client stories).
#### 14. Stories Sell: Storyworthy Strategies to Grow Your Business and Brand
- **Author:** Matthew Dicks
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=os0DEQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Applies storytelling principles to marketing copy, conversations, sales pitches and presentations.
  2. Shows the ingredients of a compelling business story.
  3. Any client story needs written consent and Susmit's approval; never fabricate one.

### Data privacy and data protection for a worldwide online coaching business (not legal advice)
#### 15. GDPR For Dummies
- **Author:** Suzanne Dibble
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=68C_DwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Step-by-step GDPR compliance for small business owners.
  2. GDPR also applies to businesses outside the EU that process personal data of people in the EU, so it reaches a worldwide coaching business.
  3. Covers marketing, service delivery and using service providers; a lawyer confirms anything final.
#### 16. The EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR): A Practical Guide (2nd ed.)
- **Author:** Paul Voigt, Axel von dem Bussche
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=agEuEQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Practical overview of GDPR requirements with examples, tables and checklists.
  2. Updated for court and supervisory-authority decisions since GDPR came into force.
  3. Reference for scope, organisational duties and data-subject rights.
#### 17. The California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA): An Implementation Guide
- **Author:** Preston Bukaty
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=RQ3XzgEACAAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Implementation guide to California's consumer privacy law (catalogue gives no description; summary from title and subtitle).
  2. Reminder that US clients bring different privacy rules from EU/UK ones.
  3. Background for a market-by-market privacy question list for a lawyer.
#### 18. Privacy is Power: Why and How You Should Take Back Control of Your Data
- **Author:** Carissa Véliz
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=ZOrSDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Accessible case on how the data economy erodes privacy and why it matters.
  2. Explains everyday data harvesting through apps and devices.
  3. Sets the tone: collect only what coaching needs and be open with clients about it.
#### 19. Information Privacy Engineering and Privacy by Design
- **Author:** William Stallings
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=chrCDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Guide to privacy by design and privacy engineering, both referenced by GDPR and ISO/IEC 27701.
  2. Covers privacy threats, controls and management processes.
  3. Background for choosing and configuring the tools that hold client data (forms, video, email, storage).
#### 20. Confidentiality, Privacy, and Data Protection in Biomedicine: International Concepts and Issues
- **Author:** Edward S. Dove (ed.)
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=jYQZEQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Scholarly volume on health-information privacy across the US, UK, Europe, South Africa and Australia.
  2. Health information raises extra legal and ethical questions about use and consent.
  3. Relevant because intake forms collect injury, medical and pregnancy details.

### Web accessibility and inclusive design
#### 21. Accessibility for Everyone
- **Author:** Laura Kalbag
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=zveutAEACAAJ
- **Takeaways:**

#### 22. Mismatch: How Inclusion Shapes Design

#### 23. Practical Web Accessibility: A Comprehensive Guide to Digital Inclusion (2nd ed.)
- **Author:** Ashley Firth
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=DeoXEQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Hands-on guide to a broad range of disabilities and access needs on the web.
  2. Notes most popular websites have accessibility issues and legal cases are increasing (treat figures as the author's).
  3. Practical fixes the site bot can check before each release.
#### 24. Inclusive Design for Accessibility: A Practical Guide to Digital Accessibility, UX, and Inclusive Web and App Design
- **Author:** Dale Cruse, Denis Boudreau
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=qaNxEQAAQBAJ

## 2. Market-by-market questions for a lawyer

These are **questions only**. They do not state what the law requires. Official pages listed against each market were opened on 14 November 2026 (see Appendix B) and are pointers for the lawyer, not conclusions. They extend Coaching Standards v1, section 8, question 3 ("Data protection").

### 2.0 Questions for every market

1. Where is Online Fitness Worldwide legally based for data-protection purposes, and who is the "controller" / "data fiduciary" / "business" — Susmit personally, or a company?
2. Does the law of each client's or prospect's country apply to us even though we are based elsewhere (because we offer coaching to, email, or monitor people there)?
3. Is the information in an intake form, a WhatsApp chat, a session note, or a "what should change" drop-down "health data" or "sensitive data" in this market? Is pregnancy status? Is a body or progress photo? Is a session video?
4. What lawful basis or consent do we need for (a) coaching delivery, (b) health screening, (c) recording sessions, (d) using photos/testimonials, (e) marketing emails to enquirers, (f) cold emails to people who never contacted us?
5. What must our privacy notice contain here, and in which languages?
6. Do we need to appoint a representative, data protection officer, or register with a regulator?
7. What rules apply to sending data abroad — to Google, Meta (WhatsApp), Zoom, Apple, email providers and AI providers?
8. What breach-notification duties apply, and to whom, within what time?
9. How long may or must we keep each record type in section 5? Are there any minimum retention duties (e.g. tax, insurance, incident records)?
10. What rights do individuals have (access, correction, deletion, withdrawal of consent, objection to marketing), and what response deadlines apply?
11. Are cookie/tracker rules (including the analytics tag found on the site) triggered here, and what consent, if any, is needed before the tag loads?
12. What do we need in contracts with providers that handle data for us (processors/service providers)?

### 2.1 EU (GDPR) and UK (UK GDPR, PECR)

- Does offering live coaching to people in the EU or UK bring us within GDPR / UK GDPR territorial scope (EDPB Guidelines 3/2018)? Do we need an EU and/or UK representative?
- Which Article 9 condition (or UK equivalent) would cover health, injury and pregnancy information collected for coaching? Is explicit consent the right route, and what must the consent form say? (ICO special category data guidance.)
- Is the GDPR consent standard (freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous, withdrawable — EDPB Guidelines 05/2020) met by a WhatsApp "yes", a ticked box on a form, or a spoken "OK" at the start of a recorded session?
- Is our cold outreach to UK/EU individuals permissible at all under PECR / national e-privacy rules, and does it matter whether the recipient is a sole trader or an employee of a company?
- Do we need a cookie consent banner before the analytics tag loads for UK and EU visitors (ICO cookies guidance)?
- What transfer mechanism covers sending EU/UK data to US-based tools (EU–US Data Privacy Framework participation of each provider; UK international-transfer rules)?
- Do we need a Data Protection Impact Assessment for health data plus video plus AI tools?
- What does the ICO's AI guidance mean for using AI tools to draft client communications?

### 2.2 United States (federal, California CCPA/CPRA, other states)

- Do we meet any CCPA/CPRA applicability threshold as a small coaching business (California AG CCPA page; CPPA)? If not now, at what point would we?
- If CCPA applies, does health/pregnancy data count as "sensitive personal information", and do we need a "Limit the Use" option or notice at collection?
- Do state consumer-health-data laws (for example Washington's My Health My Data Act, per the Washington AG page) apply to fitness and injury information collected from residents of those states, regardless of our size?
- Does HIPAA apply to us at all, given we are not a health-care provider or health plan? (Question only — we assume nothing.)
- Does every cold or nurture email meet CAN-SPAM (FTC guidance: accurate header, non-deceptive subject, identified as an ad, valid physical postal address, working opt-out honoured within 10 business days)? What postal address can we use?
- Are there state laws on recording video calls (one-party vs all-party consent) that affect recorded sessions with clients in particular states?

### 2.3 Australia (Privacy Act 1988, Australian Privacy Principles, Spam Act)

- Does the Privacy Act apply to us given the small-business exemption and the fact that we handle health information? (OAIC APP pages.)
- Which APPs bite on collection of health information (consent), cross-border disclosure (APP 8) and notice of collection (APP 5)?
- Does our outreach to Australian recipients, including GitHub users found by location, comply with the Spam Act (consent, identification, unsubscribe)?
- Are there any pending Privacy Act reforms we should design for now?

### 2.4 Middle East

**UAE (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 — PDPL; free zones such as DIFC/ADGM have their own regimes)**
- Does the PDPL apply to us as a business outside the UAE processing data of UAE residents, and is the executive regulation in force so that obligations are live?
- Is consent the only practical basis for marketing and health data under the PDPL for us? What form must it take?
- Does the UAE consumer-protection law (cited on the UAE government portal as restricting use of consumer data for marketing) affect cold outreach to UAE prospects?
- Do health-sector ICT rules (Federal Law No. 2 of 2019) apply to a fitness coach collecting health details, or only to health-service providers?
- Would a client in a free zone (DIFC/ADGM) be covered by that zone's law instead?

**Saudi Arabia (Personal Data Protection Law, SDAIA)**

## 6. The 10-point privacy check (every bot, before a draft reaches Susmit)

Run **after** Coaching Standards' claims checklist and Brand Studio's brand checklist. A draft that fails any item is fixed or flagged — never "fixed" by inventing anything.

1. **No real person's private details in public drafts.** No client name, photo, quote, story, result, location or health detail appears in any content, pitch or example unless written consent **and** Susmit's approval are on file (cite where).
2. **No health data outside its place.** Injury, condition, medicine, pregnancy and mental-health details stay out of social, video, community, outreach and email drafts, and out of file names and subject lines.
3. **No client data into AI tools** beyond what section 7 allows. If the draft was built from client notes, they were de-identified first.
4. **Minimum data.** The draft and any working file collect or store only what the task needs; no new "personal notes" or `.bak` copies of personal data.
5. **Marketing emails have an easy opt-out** (unsubscribe link or "reply stop"), a reason the person is getting it, and the placeholder for a postal address until Susmit supplies one.
6. **Opt-outs and the do-not-send list are respected.** Every recipient checked against `/workspace/outreach/do_not_send.txt` (or its successor) before a draft list goes to Susmit.
7. **Recording and images:** no draft assumes a session was or will be recorded, or uses a screenshot or clip of a client, without signed consent; faceless by default (Brand Guidelines).
8. **No new collection without a notice.** Any new form, field, sign-up, community or tracker is flagged to Privacy & Data Care so the privacy notice and consents can be updated **before** it goes live. (No analytics proposals.)
9. **Market check.** If the draft targets people in a specific country, note it — so Susmit can see whether that market's lawyer question (section 2) is still open.
10. **Honest privacy promises and clean brand.** The draft makes no privacy promise the team cannot keep (e.g. "we never store anything"), never uses the retired brand name, and states no legal position as settled.

## 7. AI-tool data rule (draft)

> **Draft for Susmit and a lawyer.** Applies to every bot and to Susmit when using AI tools (drafting, research, transcription, image or video generation).

### 7.1 Never put into an AI tool

1. Health information about an identifiable person — injuries, conditions, medicines, pregnancy/postpartum, mental health, symptoms from a session.
2. Session recordings, live-session screenshots, or audio of clients.
3. Client photos, body/progress photos, or images of anyone who has not consented to that specific AI use.
4. Emergency contacts, home or training addresses, phone numbers, dates of birth, payment details, ID documents.
5. Whole WhatsApp chats, email threads or intake forms from clients.
6. Anything about a safeguarding concern or a disclosure of harm (flag to Susmit only — Coaching Standards section 5).
7. Information about under-18s.
8. Passwords, access tokens or account logins.

### 7.2 Allowed, with care

- Drafting general content, templates and emails with **placeholders** ("[Client name]", "[injury the client named]") instead of real details.
- Summarising **de-identified** notes (no name, no contact details, no rare details that could identify the person) to plan a session — **only if Susmit approves this use** (open question).
- Researching prospects for outreach **only** from public professional pages, **only** for markets where a lawyer has confirmed it is allowed, and storing only the minimum in section 5.1.

### 7.3 Disclosure

- The privacy notice says AI tools help draft content and emails, and that health data, recordings and photos are not put into them (section 3).
- Clients are told a person reviews anything before it reaches them.
- Any AI-generated image or voice that could be mistaken for a real client or a real result is not used (consistent with "no invented testimonials").
- **Open for the lawyer:** whether specific markets require more detailed disclosure of AI use, of the AI provider, or of overseas transfer to AI providers.

### 7.4 Before any new AI tool is used with client-related work

- [ ] Susmit has approved the tool.
