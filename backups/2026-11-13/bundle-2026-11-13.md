## 1. Brand platform

Written as a proposal, grounded in the live site’s current copy and in the research knowledge base. Open questions that this section does not settle are collected in section 8.

### 1.1 Purpose — why we exist

**Proposed.** Grounded in the live homepage coach section (checked 13 November 2026): people who have already tried to train alone; the week gets in the way; the session goes vague; no one notices when it stops. The work on the call is watched. If the week is a bad one, the person still trains. The job is the joint, the line of the lift, and a load that belongs to that person today.

**Proposed purpose:**

> Online Fitness Worldwide exists so that a person anywhere in the world can train under a coach who is actually on the call — watching the set, correcting the rep while they are in it, and changing the work from what is seen — so strength, energy and long-term health are built with a held standard, not alone from a recording or from a plan written once and left to run.

This is a proposal. It is not yet a signed-off creed (Hanlon’s “creed” in *Primalbranding* is listed as a useful frame, not as a claim that this sentence is already the brand creed).

### 1.2 Audience

**Sourced**, from every knowledge-base entry’s audience line, the knowledge-base index, and the live site.

- People who want **live online** strength and conditioning, coached on video (the live site names Google Meet, Zoom and FaceTime).
- Offered as **1:1** and **small-group** (Open Floor). A 30-minute consultation comes before a training block is booked.
- Interests the live site currently speaks to: strength, power and athleticism (Iron Hour); lifestyle, weight management and energy (Lean Reset); everyday consistency and community (Open Floor). Structured data on the same page also lists **Return** (getting back into exercise after a long break) — see the open question on whether Return should be a visible programme card.
- The knowledge base and specialist team bots also support **hybrid-race** coaching (HYROX, DEKA, ATHX). As of this check, those races appear in the public blog, not as a named programme on the homepage. Prominence is an open question (section 8).
- Geography: **worldwide** — US, UK, Europe, Middle East, Asia, Australia. India is one market among these, never the default. The live FAQ lists coaching across IST, SGT, GST, CET and ET, booked in the client’s time zone.
- Coaching languages currently stated on the site: Hindi or English. Language *order* and IST-default wording on some pages are flagged in section 8 and in the findings at the end — they lean India-first and should be reviewed.

### 1.3 Positioning statement

**Proposed** structure, using only **sourced** reasons to believe. Frame of reference follows the live site’s own comparison: live coaching with Online Fitness Worldwide, versus a recorded class, versus training on your own.

**Proposed positioning:**

> For adults worldwide who want to get stronger, move with more ease, and stay consistent — including people training for performance, for daily energy and long-term health, or (where relevant) for a hybrid race — Online Fitness Worldwide is the live online strength and conditioning coach who is on the screen for the set, in a 1:1 or small-group hour, rather than a recorded class or a self-written programme: the plan is shaped session by session from the person’s goals, feedback, lifestyle and equipment; the rep is corrected in the session, in real time; check-ins are weekly (or more often if needed); and the hour is booked in the client’s time zone.

**Reasons to believe — sourced operational facts only** (live homepage and its structured data, 13 November 2026). These are descriptions of how the service is said to work, not results and not statistics:

1. Coaching is live on video, not a recorded class. The site’s own line: “The plan changes if the rep does.”
2. The rep is corrected **in the session, in real time**. The first two minutes of every session are for the camera frame; if the joint cannot be seen, the phone is moved before the lift.
3. The plan is written **with the client**, session by session, from goals, feedback and lifestyle — not a single plan left to run, and not the same plan for everyone.
4. Equipment is adjusted to what the person has: home, gym, bands, dumbbells, a backpack, or a gym.
5. Check-ins are weekly, or more often if needed. The coach is the person the client reaches out to.
6. A 30-minute consultation happens before any training block is booked. It settles the aim, the camera, and which plan fits. It ends with a clear next step, not a workout.
7. Hours are offered across named world time zones (IST, SGT, GST, CET and ET) and booked in the client’s zone. Sessions are 60 minutes for the 1:1 and small-group hours described on the homepage.
8. Named current programmes on the visible homepage: Iron Hour, Lean Reset, Open Floor, plus Consultation. (Return is in the page’s structured data and in knowledge-base offer lists, but was not a visible programme card on this check.)

**Not used as reasons to believe in this draft:**

### 1.4 Brand personality

**Proposed.** Five traits, each as “this, not that.” Derived from the live site’s existing coach voice (short, concrete, about the lift and the hour) and from the knowledge-base / Coaching Standards guardrails (worldwide, performance and health first, no appearance-shaming, no pressure-selling).

1. **Direct, not harsh.** Say the thing in a short line. “The plan changes if the rep does.” Hold the standard. Do not shout, mock, or shame a body or a missed week.
2. **Precise, not jargon-heavy.** Talk about the joint, the line of the lift, the frame, the load that belongs to this person today. Explain in plain English a reader in any of the named regions can follow. Do not hide behind unexplained abbreviations or insider slang.
3. **Present, not hype.** The brand lives in the session: the set being watched, the plan changing with the week. Do not use fake urgency, countdown scarcity, miracle claims, or “new body” language.
4. **Steady, not drill-sergeant.** Consistency and a held standard, not intimidation or bootcamp theatrics. Encouragement is calm and specific.
5. **Worldwide and respectful, not local-insider.** Address a global audience first. Never default to one country, one currency, or one festival calendar. Hindi is one coaching language, not the identity of the brand. Performance and health come before appearance.

### 1.5 Brand promise

**Proposed.** The live homepage already leads with a close cousin of this line, used here as a starting point, not as a locked tagline (tagline sign-off is an open question):

> **Your coach, live on your screen.** We watch the set and correct it while you are in it. The work changes from what we see, not from what was decided before you picked up the bar.

Shorter **proposed** promise, if a single sentence is needed:

> We are on the screen for the set, and the work changes from what we see.

### 1.6 Core messaging pillars

**Proposed.** Four pillars, each usable across the site, social, video and email. They should stay consistent; they should not be padded with unsourced proof.

1. **Live, in the session.** A coach is on the call. The rep is corrected while the person is in it. This is not a recorded class and not a PDF.
2. **The plan is yours.** Session by session, from goals, feedback, lifestyle and the equipment in the room. The hour has a clear shape (set-up, warm-up, primary lift, accessories, cool-down); the work inside it still changes.
3. **Performance and health first.** Strength, power, energy, movement quality, and the capacity to keep doing what the person loves. No before/after framing, no appearance-shaming, no implied typical results.
4. **Your hour, worldwide.** The hour is booked in the client’s time zone. The audience is global. Showing up is coached — 1:1 or in a small group — so the week does not quietly erase the work.

## 3. Tone-of-voice guide

**Proposed**, aligned with the live site’s existing short coach lines and with Social’s “short coach voice” drafts. Plain English for a global audience. Short sentences. One idea at a time. The customer is the one doing the work; the brand is the coach on the call (this sits comfortably with the StoryBrand-style reading in knowledge-base entry 3, without copying any handbook’s interior list).

### 3.1 Voice principles (every channel)

**Do**

- Write as the coach in the session: concrete, calm, specific.
- Prefer short lines. Cut any sentence that only adds atmosphere.
- Use “we” for the coaching practice and “you” for the person training, as the live site does.
- Describe the work: the frame, the lift, the week, the plan that changes.
- Keep English readable for a second-language reader. Avoid idioms that only work in one country.
- Put performance and health first. Speak about strength, energy, consistency, movement quality, and what the person wants to keep doing.
- Leave a clear next step when one is needed (consultation, a blog post, a question) — without pressure.

**Do not**

- Invent results, testimonials, quotes, or statistics. If a number was not opened in a real source this turn, it comes out (Coaching Standards, claims checklist).
- Publish prices, discounts, or payment terms.
- Imply that any coaching body endorses, accredits or partners with the business.
- Use before/after, “new body,” body-shaming, or appearance-as-worth language.
- Default to one country, “on IST” as the only clock, or Hindi-first wording that makes the brand feel local rather than worldwide. If languages are mentioned, prefer “English or Hindi, and we book the hour in your time zone,” unless Susmit sets a different order.
- Use fake urgency, fake scarcity, or fake social proof.
- Offer youth coaching or speak as if under-18s are a current client group.
- Give medical, legal or nutrition-therapy advice. Hand those topics to the right teammate and to a qualified professional, per Coaching Standards.

### 4.6 Rules for carousels, thumbnails, PDFs and email headers

Apply Robin Williams’s four principles from *The Non-Designer’s Design Book* (knowledge-base entry 9; catalogue takeaway, not a quotation of interior pages): **contrast, repetition, alignment, proximity**. This is a shared vocabulary for every bot that lays out a graphic. Final design sign-off stays with Susmit.

**Contrast**

- Put ink (`#111`) or white type on a ground that clearly holds it. On blue (`#0050f8`), use white type. On black, use white type. Do not put mid-grey on cream, and do not bring cream back.
- One accent: blue `#0050f8`. Do not add a second accent (no copper, no WhatsApp green as decoration, no red except a true warning in a safety context).
- Size and weight contrast for the hook line versus the supporting line. One hook per slide.

**Repetition**

- Same typeface (Outfit), same blue, same ink, same corner language (pills or 16px cards) across a carousel, a thumbnail set, a PDF and an email header.
- Repeat the current wordmark or favicon-style mark consistently, at a size that stays legible. Do not invent a new lockup per channel.
- Repeat a short brand line only if it is an approved line (for now, the live “Your coach, live on your screen” or the proposed promise) — do not invent a fresh slogan for each asset.

**Alignment**

- Pick one edge and keep it. On wide layouts, left-align body text, as the desktop site does. Centre only where the live tablet rules centre (headings and CTAs in that band), or on a single-hook story slide that is deliberately centred — not a mix of both on one slide.
- Align marks, rules and type to a shared grid. Do not place a logo at an arbitrary angle.

**Proximity**

- Group the things that belong together: hook with its one supporting line; programme name with its one-line frame; a CTA alone at the end, not mid-paragraph.
- Leave open space. The live site is not dense. Crowded slides are off-brand.
- Keep captions close to the image they explain; keep the wordmark away from body copy enough to breathe.

**Channel-specific notes (proposed)**

- **IG carousels:** square; white ground; ink type; thin blue rule if a rule is needed; first slide is the hook only. 9:16 for Reels. No credential logos. No before/after.
- **YouTube thumbnails:** one face only if Susmit has approved face-forward content; otherwise a clear lift or setup photo, a short Outfit hook (few words), blue or white rule, wordmark small in a corner. Do not fake playback counters or “UPVOTED” badges.
- **PDFs (guides, primers, onboarding):** 72rem-inspired measure (a readable max width); Outfit; white ground; blue for headings rules and links; page numbers; the full brand name in the header, never the retired name. No prices. No invented charts.
- **Email headers:** simple, light, and light-weight in file size. Wordmark on white, optional thin blue rule, no image-heavy mastheads that break in dark-mode mail clients. Plain-text name in the from-line: a person at Online Fitness Worldwide.

## 5. Imagery rules

**Sourced** in principle from Coaching Standards v1 (consent and confidentiality; claims), from knowledge-base entry 9’s body-image reading (performance and health first; no appearance-shaming; no before/after), and from the live site’s existing process photography. **Proposed** as the operational rules every bot applies.

**Do**

- Lead with performance and health: a lift being coached, a camera set at the side, a clear frame, a room someone could actually train in, a small group at work, a hybrid-race *station skill* taught as movement — not as a trophy.
- Prefer process over glamour. The live “hour” images (set-up, warm-up, primary, accessories, cool-down) are the right idea.
- Show worldwide-plausible rooms and kit (a phone, a mat, bands, a bar, a backpack, a gym corner). Do not code the images to one country.
- Crop faces out unless Susmit has approved face-forward content for that channel. Pre-launch Social work was intentionally faceless; that remains a safe default for drafts.
- Use the live palette around images (white, ink, black, blue). A thin blue or white rule is enough.
- Alt text in plain English, with the current brand name if the mark is in the frame, never the retired name.

**Do not**

- **Before/after images**, slider comparisons, or “progress” pairs that shame a starting body. This is a hard rule.
- **Appearance-shaming** of any kind: circled “fat,” exaggerated wide-angle bodies, “fix your belly” crops, or comedy at the expense of how someone looks.
- **Client photographs, videos, voices, names, quotes, results or stories** in any public or outreach piece without **written consent from the client and approval from Susmit**. Both are required (Coaching Standards, section 3). If consent is missing, unclear, or only verbal, do not use a placeholder story. Draft a request for Susmit instead.
- **Invented people.** No stock-photo model presented as a client. No composite “typical member.”
- **Coaching-body logos** (or any other organisation’s mark) used as a lockup, badge or “as certified by” graphic. This includes marks currently on the live coach section — do not extend that pattern. See section 8.
- **The retired former brand name** on a graphic, a watermark, or an old letterhead.
- **Youth imagery** that implies we coach under-18s.
- Medical before/after, scan images, or clinical settings that make the brand look like a clinic.
- Flags as a substitute for a worldwide audience, or a single national landmark as if it were “home.”

If a complaint or a critical comment arrives with an image, drafts go to Susmit; bots do not reply in public (knowledge-base entry 9, complaints reading; Community and Social draft only).

## 6. Brand-consistency checklist (10 points)

Every bot runs this list **before** a draft reaches Susmit. It complements, and does not replace, the claims checklist in Coaching Standards v1 (section 4 of that file). A draft that fails any item is revised or flagged. It is not “fixed” by inventing a source, a number, a colour, or a story.

1. **Name.** The only brand name in the draft is OnlineFitnessWorldwide or Online Fitness Worldwide. The retired former brand name does not appear in body, title, metadata, filename suggestion, alt text or graphic.
2. **Worldwide framing.** The audience is worldwide. India is one market, never the default, never the only example, never the first time zone or first language as if it were the home market. Time zones, when listed, stay a set (IST, SGT, GST, CET, ET, or the client’s own), not “on IST” alone.
3. **Voice.** Short coach lines, plain English, performance and health first. No hype, no appearance-shaming, no before/after, no pressure-selling, no fake urgency.
4. **Visuals.** New graphics use the **live** palette (white, ink `#111`, black, blue `#0050f8`), Outfit (and Noto Sans Devanagari only when the script needs it), and the contrast / repetition / alignment / proximity rules in section 4. Cream/bone palettes and decorative WhatsApp green are not used. Reduced motion is respected where animation is proposed.
5. **Imagery and consent.** No client photo, story, quote or result without written consent and Susmit’s approval. No invented people. No credential-body logos. No youth-as-clients imagery.
6. **Claims.** No invented facts, statistics, results or testimonials. Every research number is from a source that was actually opened. No cure or treatment claims. This item is the bridge to Coaching Standards’ full claims checklist — run that list as well.
7. **No implied endorsement.** The draft does not say or imply that ASCA, ACE, ACSM, NSCA, or any other body endorses, accredits or partners with OnlineFitnessWorldwide. Credentials, if Susmit has approved a wording, are stated as the coach’s qualifications in words, not as a logo lockup.
8. **No prices.** No fees, discounts, currency amounts, or payment terms. Programme names and session lengths may be described as the approved site copy does. Pricing stays with Susmit.
9. **Offers and scope.** Programme names match the current offer set (and do not resurrect a retired name). Hybrid-race content is not invented; it is tied to work the team actually does and, for public posts, to a real page or an approved brief. Youth coaching is not offered. Clinical topics are handed on, not answered as a clinician.
10. **Draft only.** Nothing is published, posted, sent or messaged by a bot. Susmit reviews first.

### Habit science and behaviour change for adherence

#### 1. Good Habits, Bad Habits: The Science of Making Positive Changes That Stick
- **Author:** Wendy Wood
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=t-6fDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Research-led case that roughly half of daily behaviour runs on habit, not willpower.
  2. Shape context and cues (friction, repetition, rewards) so training happens without heroic motivation.
  3. Useful for onboarding and check-in design: make the session time, place and kit setup automatic.

#### 2. The Power of Habit: Why We Do What We Do, and How to Change
- **Author:** Charles Duhigg
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=c12idXqsE5cC
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Popular explanation of the cue-routine-reward loop and keystone habits.
  2. Exercise is framed as a keystone habit that spills into other behaviours; keep that as an idea, not a promise.
  3. Story-led, easy content source for Social and Nurture.

#### 3. Switch: How to Change Things When Change Is Hard
- **Author:** Chip Heath, Dan Heath
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=XmzbeoN-KZ0C
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Rider-Elephant-Path model: give clear direction, engage emotion, shape the environment.
