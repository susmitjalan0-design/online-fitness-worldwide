## Books (28)

### Community, belonging and member experience (priority gap)

#### 1. Get Together
- **Author:** Bailey Richardson, Kevin Huynh, Kai Elmer Sotto
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=BRnBEAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Communities start with a small core of people who already care, doing something together — not a big launch announcement.
  2. Grow by handing members real roles (hosts, buddies, welcomers) rather than the coach doing everything.
  3. Blueprint for turning Open Floor small-group sessions into a member community across time zones.

#### 2. The Art of Community (2nd ed.)
- **Author:** Charles H. Vogl
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=vFYiEQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Vogl's principles (boundary, initiation, rituals, temple, stories, symbols, inner rings) give a community a shape people can feel.
  2. Rituals — a welcome, a race-week send-off, a block-completion moment — matter more than platform choice.
  3. Clear values and boundaries keep a worldwide group friendly without heavy moderation.

#### 3. The Business of Belonging
- **Author:** David Spinks
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=5KEkEAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Community is a business asset only when it creates value for members first.
  2. Spinks' SPACES model (support, product ideas, acquisition, contribution, engagement, success) forces you to name one primary job for the community.
  3. For Online Fitness Worldwide the primary job is client success and belonging; acquisition stays a by-product, not the pitch.

#### 4. Buzzing Communities
- **Author:** Richard Millington
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=KHajMQEACAAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Online communities move through life-cycle stages; tactics that suit 15 members fail at 150.
  2. Strategy, growth, content, events and moderation each need an owner — otherwise the group goes quiet.
  3. Plan regular, predictable activity instead of hoping members post on their own.

#### 5. The Art of Gathering
- **Author:** Priya Parker
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=jyJJDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Every gathering needs a specific purpose, not 'a catch-up'.
  2. Hosts should use 'generous authority' — set norms, protect the group, do not stay passive.
  3. Strong openings and closings make a 60-minute live group session or a virtual race-day meetup memorable.

#### 6. The Culture Code
- **Author:** Daniel Coyle
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=YkcyCwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. High-performing groups build belonging cues, shared vulnerability and a clear purpose.
  2. Coaches who admit their own misses make it safe for clients to report missed sessions honestly.
  3. Useful for Open Floor culture and for Mindset's group-confidence language.

#### 7. Tribes
- **Author:** Seth Godin
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=vD4q8qj1dtkC
- **Takeaways:**
  1. A tribe needs a shared idea and a way for members to talk to each other, not only to the leader.
  2. Leadership in a small coaching brand means connecting members around a mission (stronger, race-ready, consistent).
  3. Background for community positioning; never use 'tribe' hype to imply invented results.

#### 8. Customer Communities
- **Author:** Nick Mehta, Robin Van Lieshout
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=avjgEAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Customer communities support retention when they help members succeed, not when they are a marketing channel.
  2. Peer-to-peer help reduces the load on the coach and makes members feel known.
  3. Ties community work back to Retention's success outcomes without duplicating 1:1 check-ins.

#### 9. The Power of Moments
- **Author:** Chip Heath, Dan Heath
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=cFU3DwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Memorable experiences come from moments of elevation, insight, pride and connection.
  2. Mark transitions (first session, first race entry, block completion) instead of letting them pass silently.
  3. Gives Community, Retention and Video a menu of milestone moments to design — celebrated only with member consent.

#### 10. Unreasonable Hospitality
- **Author:** Will Guidara
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=UqxWEAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Service is what you do; hospitality is how people feel while you do it.
  2. Small, specific, unexpected gestures build loyalty more than big generic perks.
  3. Raises the bar for live online sessions: remember details, personalise, follow through.

### Client lifecycle and low-effort service

#### 11. Never Lose a Customer Again
- **Author:** Joey Coleman
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=zMsvDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. The first 100 days decide whether a new client stays; Coleman maps them as phases from assess through advocate.
  2. Plan deliberate touchpoints in the first weeks rather than going quiet after the sale.
  3. Upgrades Retention's Day 0–30 onboarding flow and gives Community a 'welcome into the group' moment.

#### 12. The Effortless Experience
- **Author:** Matthew Dixon, Nicholas Toman, Rick DeLisi
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=9mzbBQ2wVdEC
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Reducing customer effort drives loyalty more reliably than trying to 'delight' at every turn.
  2. Anticipate the next question (time zones, rescheduling, camera set-up) before the client has to ask.
  3. Site, Nurture and Retention should remove friction from booking, rescheduling and session joining.

#### 13. Customer Success
- **Author:** Nick Mehta, Dan Steinman, Lincoln Murphy
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=QY2XCwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Customer success is proactive: help clients reach the outcome they came for before they think about leaving.
  2. Segment clients by need, not just by programme name.
  3. Background for Retention and Community hand-offs; no metrics bot needed to apply it.

### Email lifecycle and clear writing

#### 14. Email Marketing Rules (2023 ed.)
- **Author:** Chad S. White
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=ZRvw0QEACAAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Subscriber-first email: permission, relevance, sensible frequency and easy unsubscribe protect deliverability and trust.
  2. Lifecycle emails (welcome, onboarding, re-engagement) need triggers and clear ownership.
  3. Upgrades Nurture's sequences and Sydney's opt-out discipline; no new email bot needed yet.

#### 15. The Copywriter's Handbook (4th ed.)
- **Author:** Robert W. Bly
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=GSKaDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Lead with the reader's benefit, then back it with specific, true features.
  2. Headlines and subject lines do most of the work — make them clear before clever.
  3. Never trade accuracy for punch: no invented results, no prices in coaching copy.

#### 16. Writing for Busy Readers
- **Author:** Todd Rogers, Jessica Lasky-Fink
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=-Wu6EAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Busy readers skim: less text, easy navigation, formatting only where it helps.
  2. Tell readers why the message matters to them and make responding easy.
  3. Directly applicable to check-ins, community announcements, cold emails and blog intros.

#### 17. On Writing Well (30th anniversary ed.)
- **Author:** William Zinsser
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=R-85PhmkW5gC
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Clarity, simplicity and cutting clutter beat jargon.
  2. Write for one real reader; strip filler words from every draft.
  3. A shared style baseline for the site, Social, Nurture and Sydney.

### Running economy, intensity control and tapering for hybrid races

#### 18. 80/20 Running
- **Author:** Matt Fitzgerald
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=5eVhAwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Most running volume should feel easy; a smaller share is genuinely hard.
  2. Hybrid-race clients often run every session at a grey middle effort — 80/20 fixes that.
  3. Hybrid Race Coach can apply this to the running that sits between HYROX / DEKA / ATHX stations.

#### 19. Running Science
- **Author:** Owen Anderson
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=fvJ6DwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Evidence-based look at running physiology, economy and training methods.
  2. Running economy is trainable through strength, technique and specific practice, not only more miles.
  3. Helps Hybrid Race Coach explain why strength work belongs in a race block.

#### 20. Running Rewired (2nd ed.)
- **Author:** Jay Dicharry
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=sEX8EAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Running form improves through stability, mobility and strength drills, not just cueing.
  2. Short movement-prep drills fit Susmit's general warm-up style.
  3. Video can film drill demos; Physio checks any pain-related language.

#### 21. Heart Rate Training
- **Author:** Roy Benson, Declan Connolly
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=U1TADwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Heart-rate zones individualise intensity for clients training remotely without a coach beside them.
  2. Use HR alongside perceived effort; devices and formulas vary.
  3. Gives online clients a simple way to keep easy days easy.

#### 22. Tapering and Peaking for Optimal Performance
- **Author:** Iñigo Mujika
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=CbNxygAACAAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Tapering usually means cutting volume while keeping intensity and some frequency.
  2. Race-week mistakes (new sessions, sudden rest, extra volume) cost more than they gain.
  3. Hybrid Race Coach owns taper weeks before HYROX / DEKA / ATHX; Masters adapts them for 40+.

#### 23. The Complete Guide to Indoor Rowing
- **Author:** Jim Flood, Charles Simpson
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=Ua-OCBHAUqoC
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Rowing technique follows a legs–body–arms sequence on the drive and the reverse on the recovery.
  2. Damper setting is not 'difficulty' — technique and power matter more.
  3. Directly useful for erg stations in hybrid races and for Video technique clips.

### Kettlebell, bodyweight and station conditioning

#### 24. Kettlebell Training
- **Author:** Steve Cotter
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=-XonAQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Kettlebell swings, cleans and carries build power endurance and grip with minimal kit.
  2. Progress technique before load or volume, especially for the swing hinge.
  3. Ideal for online clients with limited equipment; supports devil-press-style whiteboard sessions.

#### 25. Bodyweight Strength Training Anatomy
- **Author:** Bret Contreras
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=5Qt5AAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Illustrated bodyweight progressions with clear regress/progress options.
  2. Lets online clients train push, pull, squat, hinge and core without a full gym.
  3. Good source for Video demos and travelling-client plans.

#### 26. Strength and Conditioning for Team Sports
- **Author:** Paul Gamble
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=G-BzDGM8s30C
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Start conditioning from a needs analysis: what does the event actually demand?
  2. Repeated high-effort work with incomplete recovery is its own quality to train.
  3. Transfers to sled push/pull, carries and wall-ball stations in hybrid races.

### Pain education and how people learn

#### 27. Explain Pain (2nd ed.)
- **Author:** David S. Butler, G. Lorimer Moseley
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=e59-QdDArXkC
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Pain is a protective output, not a precise measure of tissue damage.
  2. Plain-language pain education can reduce fear of movement — but it is Physio/clinician-led, not a coach diagnosis.
  3. Physio reviews any pain wording before it ships; persistent or unexplained pain goes to a qualified clinician.

#### 28. Make It Stick
- **Author:** Peter C. Brown, Henry L. Roediger III, Mark A. McDaniel
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=fpAXEQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Retrieval practice, spacing and interleaving make learning stick better than re-reading.
  2. Applies to teaching movement: ask clients to recall cues, spread skill practice across the week.
  3. Upgrades onboarding education, Video series design and community challenge formats.
