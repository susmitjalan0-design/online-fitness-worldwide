## Books (28)

### Women's health, pregnancy and postpartum (priority gap)

#### 1. Exercising Through Your Pregnancy
- **Author:** James F. Clapp, Catherine Cram
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=-tfYTNdULpUC
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Research-led case that most healthy pregnancies can continue regular exercise, with adjustments rather than stopping.
  2. Useful frame for client conversations: the clinician clears, the coach adapts load, intensity and position.
  3. Source material for a pre/postnatal FAQ, always paired with a 'check with your midwife or doctor' line.

#### 2. Exercise and Physical Activity During Pregnancy and Postpartum: Evidence-Based Guidelines
- **Author:** Rita Santos-Rocha (ed.)
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=WV2YEAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Academic, multi-author collection summarising international guidelines for exercise in pregnancy and after birth.
  2. Good for keeping advice consistent across countries rather than following one national body.
  3. Reference stack for screening questions and red-flag symptoms that mean stop and refer.

#### 3. The Complete Guide to Postnatal Fitness
- **Author:** Judy DiFiore
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=gHslAgAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Practical guide to rebuilding fitness after birth in stages, starting with breathing, core and posture.
  2. Covers common postnatal issues coaches meet, such as abdominal separation and pelvic floor weakness.
  3. Template for a phased return-to-training framework that hands clinical questions to a physio.

#### 4. Diastasis Recti: The Whole-Body Solution to Abdominal Weakness and Separation
- **Author:** Katy Bowman
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=vQYSEQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Treats abdominal separation as a whole-body load and alignment issue, not just a crunch problem.
  2. Gives plain-language explanations clients can follow between sessions.
  3. Use as education, with assessment left to a qualified pelvic health or women's health physio.

#### 5. Run Again: A Postpartum Plan to Stop Leaking, Protect Your Pelvic Floor, and Run Again
- **Author:** Tessa Lindholm
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=GeEFEgAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Focused on returning to running after birth while protecting the pelvic floor, per its subtitle.
  2. Directly relevant for postpartum clients aiming at HYROX, DEKA or ATHX running legs.
  3. Supports a gradual run-return progression with symptom checks rather than a fixed date.

#### 6. Handbook of Sports Medicine and Science: The Female Athlete
- **Author:** Margo Mountjoy (ed.)
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=26XHBAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. IOC-published handbook on health and performance issues specific to female athletes.
  2. Covers energy availability, bone health, injury patterns and the menstrual cycle in sport.
  3. Authoritative backbone for female-athlete education content worldwide.

#### 7. The Female Athlete Triad: A Clinical Guide
- **Author:** Catherine M. Gordon, Meryl S. LeBoff (eds.)
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=h2AcBQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Clinical guide to the link between low energy availability, menstrual disruption and bone health.
  2. Helps coaches recognise warning signs early and refer, not diagnose.
  3. Natural hand-off point with Nutrition (fueling) and Physio (bone stress).

#### 8. Menstruation and the Menstrual Cycle in Sport, Exercise, and Physical Activity
- **Author:** Petra Kolić, Christopher I. Morse
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=UJzEEQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Recent (2026) academic book on how the menstrual cycle relates to training and participation.
  2. Useful for separating evidence from hype around strict 'cycle syncing'.
  3. Supports individual symptom tracking and flexible planning rather than one rule for every woman.

#### 9. Coaching Female Athletes
- **Author:** Fraser Carson, Khatija Bahdur, Tihana Nemcic Bojic
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=y42aEQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Recent (2025) book on coaching practice for women and girls in sport.
  2. Helps make communication, environment and programming genuinely inclusive.
  3. Good input for onboarding and check-in language for female clients.

#### 10. Strong Curves: A Woman's Guide to Building a Better Butt and Body
- **Author:** Bret Contreras, Kellie Davis
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=CCOREAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Popular strength programme for women built around hip thrusts, squats and progressive overload.
  2. Plain-English programme structure that can inspire beginner women's blocks.
  3. Reference for glute and hip strength that also carries over to sled and lunge stations.

#### 11. The New Rules of Lifting for Women
- **Author:** Lou Schuler, Cassandra Forsythe, Alwyn Cosgrove
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=wPJbVxkLgKYC
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Argues women should train with real loads and compound lifts, not light 'toning' work.
  2. Useful myth-busting material for social and blog content.
  3. Simple, repeatable workout structure in line with one main lift per session.

#### 12. Glute Lab: The Art and Science of Strength and Physique Training
- **Author:** Bret Contreras, Glen Cordoza
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=3E6qDwAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Deep reference on hip extension, glute training and programme design.
  2. Helps pick accessories that pair with a main lift between sets.
  3. Useful for both women's strength content and hybrid-race leg durability.

### Breathing and respiratory training

#### 13. Breath: The New Science of a Lost Art
- **Author:** James Nestor
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=a1CMEQAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Popular-science book on nasal breathing, slow breathing and breath practices.
  2. Good hook for engaging social and video content about breathing.
  3. Treat as popular science: some claims go beyond the research, so pair with stronger sources.

#### 14. The Oxygen Advantage
- **Author:** Patrick McKeown
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=7FPXBAAAQBAJ
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Promotes nasal breathing and reduced-breathing drills for fitness and calm.
  2. Gives simple drills that can sit in a warm-up or cool-down.
  3. Evidence is mixed; present as an option, never as a cure or a guaranteed performance gain.

#### 15. Respiratory Muscle Training: Theory and Practice
- **Author:** Alison McConnell
- **Google Books:** https://books.google.com/books?id=7VVPf7MAN9cC
- **Takeaways:**
  1. Academic, evidence-based guide to training the breathing muscles.
  2. Relevant for hybrid races where breathing gets hard on the rower, SkiErg and wall balls.
  3. Helps judge when breathing-muscle training is worth adding and when it isn't.

  1. Classic on matching the message to how aware the reader already is of their problem and the solution.

#### 18. Ogilvy on Advertising

  3. Good guardrail against hype in fitness marketing.

  3. Direct input for the site bot's consult and race-plan pages.
