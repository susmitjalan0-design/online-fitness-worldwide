# Books to upgrade Susmit Jalan's bots (Jalan PT / Online Fitness Worldwide)

Verification note: every ID below was returned by Google Books search for that title+author on 2026-11-02. The `googleapis.com/books/v1/volumes` endpoint returned HTTP 429 (daily quota 0 for anonymous use) from this box and from WebFetch. I used Google's public Books feed (`www.google.com/books/feeds/volumes?q=...`), which is the same catalogue and returns the same volume IDs. Link format: https://books.google.com/books?id=<ID>. Year is the edition year Google lists for that ID. Selection rests on my knowledge of each title's standing plus the catalogue check; I did not pull Goodreads or Amazon ratings.

## 1. Sydney (cold email outreach)
| # | Title | Author | Year | Link | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Influence, New and Expanded | Robert Cialdini | 2021 | https://books.google.com/books?id=4uf8DwAAQBAJ | The reference text on why people say yes. Takeaways: 1) Open with a genuine, specific give or compliment (reciprocity, liking). 2) Use real social proof, such as "other coaches in Dubai", and never invent it. 3) Ask for a small, low-friction first step (commitment). |
| 2 | Pre-Suasion | Robert Cialdini | 2016 | https://books.google.com/books?id=GI7tDAAAQBAJ | What happens before the ask decides the answer. Takeaways: 1) Make the first line prime the recipient's own goal or problem. 2) Add unity cues ("fellow coach", shared city or sport). 3) Choose the single most relevant hook per recipient. |
| 3 | Never Split the Difference | Chris Voss, Tahl Raz | 2016 | https://books.google.com/books?id=TL8CCwAAQBAJ | FBI-negotiator tactics that work well in follow-ups. Takeaways: 1) Ask "no"-oriented, low-pressure questions ("Would it be crazy to…?"). 2) Use labels and mirroring in replies ("It sounds like timing is tight"). 3) Reply to objections with calibrated "how/what" questions. |
| 4 | Fanatical Prospecting | Jeb Blount | 2015 | https://books.google.com/books?id=DByRCgAAQBAJ | Prospecting cadence and discipline, which suits 8 emails/day. Takeaways: 1) Run a multi-touch follow-up sequence (about 4–6 touches over 2–3 weeks) instead of a single send. 2) Keep a daily prospecting quota and review reply rates. 3) Make one clear ask per email. |
| 5 | New Sales. Simplified. | Mike Weinberg | 2012 | https://books.google.com/books?id=jJjEuX90SVoC | A practical new-business outreach framework. Takeaways: 1) Use a "sales story" and value proposition built on outcomes. 2) Lead with the prospect, not "we". 3) Use a short email structure of relevance, proof, then ask. |
| 6 | Cashvertising | Drew Eric Whitman | 2008 | https://books.google.com/books?id=md5EDwAAQBAJ | 100+ psychology-backed copy techniques. Takeaways: 1) Write around core desires such as fitness, status and confidence, not features. 2) Use specificity and numbers ("12 weeks, 3 sessions/week"). 3) Write subject lines that create curiosity or a concrete benefit. |
| 7 | Smart Brevity | Jim VandeHei, Mike Allen, Roy Schwartz | 2023 | https://books.google.com/books?id=EXabEAAAQBAJ | Brevity discipline for short emails. Takeaways: 1) Cut to one strong first line and one idea per email. 2) Keep emails under about 100 words, with short sentences, active verbs and no filler. 3) Use plain "why it matters to you" framing. |
| 8 | Made to Stick | Chip Heath, Dan Heath | 2008 | https://books.google.com/books?id=OUgCHkxZTX4C | Makes messages memorable. Takeaways: 1) Apply the SUCCESs checklist: simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, story. 2) Open with one concrete example, such as a client result, instead of an abstract claim. 3) Cut the "curse of knowledge" jargon. |

Deliverability note: none of these cover technical deliverability (SPF/DKIM/DMARC, warm-up, sending volume). Keep 8/day with a verified domain and plain-text style. Use Smart Brevity and Weinberg for copy, and treat Google/Gmail bulk-sender guidance as the technical source.

## 3. Grok Bot (marketing generalist + client training protocols)
| # | Title | Author | Year | Link | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Science and Practice of Strength Training (3rd ed.) | Vladimir Zatsiorsky, William Kraemer, Andrew Fry | 2020 | https://books.google.com/books?id=3v3FDwAAQBAJ | Classic text on strength theory. Takeaways: 1) Use the dose-response relationship of load, volume and intensity. 2) Choose effort methods (maximal, dynamic, repeated) by goal. 3) Manage fatigue and adaptation. |
| 2 | Science and Development of Muscle Hypertrophy (2nd ed.) | Brad Schoenfeld | 2020 | https://books.google.com/books?id=RCPHDwAAQBAJ | Evidence-based hypertrophy. Takeaways: 1) Set weekly sets per muscle (about 10–20) and train near failure (RIR 0–3). 2) Use a 6–30 rep range, provided sets are hard. 3) Train each muscle at least twice per week; weigh mechanisms against practical limits. |
| 3 | Scientific Principles of Hypertrophy Training | Michael Israetel et al. (Renaissance Periodization) | 2021 | https://books.google.com/books?id=fcVCzgEACAAJ | Programmable hypertrophy and fat-loss rules. Takeaways: 1) Use MEV/MAV/MRV volume landmarks and weekly progression through a mesocycle. 2) Plan deloads. 3) Choose exercises by stimulus-to-fatigue ratio. |
| 4 | Periodization: Theory and Methodology of Training (6th ed.) | Tudor Bompa, Carlo Buzzichelli | 2018 | https://books.google.com/books?id=xPR6DwAAQBAJ | Structures long-term plans. Takeaways: 1) Build macro/meso/micro cycles and peak for target events. 2) Use phases (prep, strength, power, taper). 3) Set a block order of training qualities. |
| 5 | The Hybrid Athlete (2nd ed.) | Alex Viada | 2025 (orig. 2019) | https://books.google.com/books?id=D4nl0AEACAAJ | Combines strength with endurance, directly useful for HYROX and ATHX. | 7 | Atomic Habits | James Clear | 2018 | https://books.google.com/books?id=fFCjDQAAQBAJ | Adherence is what drives results. Takeaways: 1) Set habit cues and stack habits onto existing routines. 2) Start with the smallest version of a habit. 3) Track and make progress visible. |
